# asset-renderer

Headless renderer for Minecraft blocks/items/entities/fluids/portals. Outputs `ImageData` (static PNG or animated frames) via `Renderer<O>` impls. Group `lib.minecraft`, package root `lib.minecraft.renderer.**`.

## Build
- JDK 21 + **Vector API incubator** (`--add-modules=jdk.incubator.vector`). Wired into JavaCompile/Test/JavaExec/JMH in `build.gradle.kts` - missing it anywhere = class-not-found at load, not silent fallback.
- Deps: Gradle Kotlin DSL, `libs.versions.toml`. Strictly-pinned jitpack snapshots from `simplified-dev` (`collections`, `utils`, `image`, `gson-extras`, `client`), `simplified-api` (`mojang`, owns the Feign `MojangContract` for client-jar / textures fetches that `AssetPipeline` proxies), and `minecraft-library` (`text`). Bump by editing the version string.
- ASM 9.8 (Java 25 class files) - `VanillaTintsLoader` parses `BlockColors` from the extracted client jar.

## Tests
- `./gradlew test` - fast tests (excludes `@Tag("slow")`).
- `./gradlew slowTest` - hits network / filesystem cache (client-jar downloads, integration, parallelism, block-entity parity). Not up-to-date-cached.

### Which gate sees what - do not cite a gate blind to your change
The suite is dense but its coverage is uneven in ways that do not follow from the file list, and each of these was measured rather than assumed.
- **`BlockRenderer` never calls `buildBox`, and both item `buildBox` call sites are `FaceTextures.uniform`.** So the block and item parity sums are *structurally blind* to a change in the box builder - a clean block sum is not evidence there. The gates that do see it are the 14 armoured entity rows, the player CRC pin, and the player contact sheets. **The ungated player surface can be byte-checked rather than eyeballed**: `./gradlew playerRender -Psheets=core-matrix,toggles,armor-per-slot` writes 31 PNGs covering BUST in both dimensions, the cape from front and rear, the elytra, both 2D composites and the overlay-off branch, so hashing them either side of a change (`git stash push -- src` between the two renders) turns a LOOK gauge into a byte comparison. This is the converse of the depth immunity recorded under Parity: blocks are immune to *depth* and reachable by *coverage*, and blind to `buildBox` for a third reason again.
- **`TestPlayerParityVanilla` asserts nothing.** It is a `main` that alpha-crops **and rescales both sides** to a common box before diffing, so it cannot detect a part-placement or fit change of any size. It renders `FULL` and `SKULL` only and `SkinOptions.renderCape` defaults false. **The player byte pin is `PlayerRasterizeFittedGoldenTest`** - two CRC32 constants, `FULL` and `SKULL`, **no cape and no armour**, and it is `@Tag("slow")` so `./gradlew test` never runs it.
- **No test pins BUST, the cape, or any 2D player path.** A change reaching them carries a purpose-built pin, lands on an announced LOOK gauge, or - cheapest and strongest of the three when the change is meant to be byte-neutral - is checked against the hashed contact sheets above.
- **`playerRender` ships NINE offline sheet groups, and any shorter `-Psheets=` list is a hole rather than a sample.** `core-matrix` / `projections` / `facing` / `toggles` / `armor-3d` / `armor-2d` / `armor-per-slot` / `leather-dye` / `trims`, plus `glint-per-slot` (GIFs) and the network-only `account`. All ten offline groups hash as **104 files** - 9 sheet composites, 85 cell PNGs and 10 glint GIFs - so that is the invocation to hash when a change's player reach is not precisely known. **The elytra and the cape appear in `toggles` alone**: a list naming the armour and trim groups but not `toggles` is blind to `ElytraKit.buildPlayerWings3D` and to both cape views, and reads as a clean pass. Which cells a given line reaches is provable rather than arguable - perturb the value and re-render the group, and the cells that move name the reach exactly (a deliberate wrong scale in the wing build's fit frame moves `elytra_only_3_4_` and `elytra_cape_3_4_` and none of the other eleven `toggles` cells).
- **The fluid and portal paths have no parity sweep, but their renders are byte-deterministic, so hash them rather than looking at them.** `./gradlew fluidRenderer` writes 12 files and `portalRenderer` 12, and both reproduce **byte for byte across two `--rerun-tasks` runs** - so for a change that is *meant* to be byte-neutral they are a real gate, not the LOOK gauge the task list makes them look like. That is the same conversion the hashed player contact sheets are, and it is worth knowing which way it runs: determinism is what makes a hash admissible, so check the two-run agreement first (`atlas.png` fails it by design and can never be hashed this way). A LOOK render stays the right tool for a change that is *supposed* to move pixels.
- **Now gated, and these three suites are the gate:** `FacePhaseTest` pins the corner phase, the winding and the position-to-UV pairing on both corner phases (nothing covered either of them before it, and `FaceTest` - the surviving direction test - covers only lookups, normals and lighting); `HumanoidPartCropTest` pins all 72 skin rectangles on both sheet formats including the legacy left-limb fallback; `FrameTurnTest` relates the three separate spellings of the armour frame turn.
- **`BlockGeometryKitTest` builds every fixture by reflection into private parser-populated fields.** Renaming one fails at *runtime* with `NoSuchFieldException`, so a rename refactor looks clean and then breaks the gate. `FrameTurnTest` carries the same trap by construction, since the three members it relates are private.
- **No parity sweep reaches the paletted trim permutation, and the item sum is not the gate it reads as.** A throw-probe on `TrimKit.permuteFrom` gets **0 hits** across all five sweeps. The item sweep renders untrimmed icons; its 18 rows whose id contains `trim` are all `*_armor_trim_smithing_template`, which are flat sprites that permute nothing. The armour sweep's seven subjects (adult + baby zombie / piglin, iron + dyed leather) carry no trim either. The two gates that do reach it are `ArmorKitCitCompositeTest` - the entity-atlas test and the both-paths-resolve-the-same-triple pin, which is the **only** thing covering the item-icon half at all - and `./gradlew playerRender -Psheets=trims`, whose 11 cells hash as a byte gate. Cite those, never the item sum.

## Sampling between ticks (`subTickSteps`)
`Timeline.bake` lives on `Timeline` (not `TickTimeline`) and reads each frame's instant off the millisecond axis, so a schedule whose instants are **off** the tick lattice can still drive the one shared bake loop. The integer tick handed to a draw is that instant floored - exact for every tick-native schedule, since the ms axis *is* the tick lattice there.
- `AnimationOptions.subTickSteps` (default `1`) subdivides each step: `Timeline.gameTime(start, frames, ticksPerFrame, subTickSteps)` returns a `SubTickLoop` covering the same span of game time at the same speed. The step's playback delay is shared across its sub-steps with the leftover milliseconds going to the earliest frames (3 steps → `17/17/16`), so a tick still spans exactly 50 ms. `subTickSteps <= 1` returns the whole-tick schedule itself, so the default path is untouched.
- **`SubTickLoop` is deliberately not a `TickTimeline`.** Off-lattice instants have no honest `tickAt`, and claiming one would break the `millisAt(f) == tickAt(f) * 50.0` identity (`TimelineTest`) that makes the lattice checkable.
- A draw that needs the fraction implements `RasterPass.ContinuousRasterizer` (`target, tick, ageInTicks`); everything else keeps the 2-arg `FrameRasterizer`. That separation is what keeps `Textures.*AtTick` on `int` - vanilla's texture interpolation is itself whole-tick, so a fraction there would be *anti*-parity.
- **Only the portal uses it.** A texture flipbook has no state between its frames, so subdividing its ticks would bake duplicates. Use it for a subject whose appearance is a continuous function of time.
- Gate: `./gradlew portalRenderer` writes `*_animated` and `*_animated_smooth` pairs. Every Nth frame of the smooth strip must be byte-identical to the plain strip's frame, and the frames between must be genuinely intermediate (≈1/3 and 2/3 of a tick's motion), not duplicates.

## Time-driven item icons (the clock)
`minecraft:time` is a normalized **sun angle**, not a linear day fraction. In 26.1 the curve is data-driven: `data/minecraft/timeline/day.json` declares a single `360 -> 0` degree keyframe pair anchored at **noon** over a `24000`-tick period, eased by a symmetric cubic Bezier `[0.362, 0.241, 0.638, 0.759]`. `option/SunAngle` reproduces it float-for-float; a linear ramp is off by more than two clock faces at sunrise.
- **Tick 0 is noon and yields exactly `+0.0f`.** That is load-bearing twice: it keeps `gui().atTick(0)` equal to `gui()`, so `ItemRenderer.resolveRenderItem`'s baked fast path survives (a `-0.0f` would silently cost *every* item its fast path and its baked tints), and it makes frame 0 the instant the item parity references are pinned at.
- `AnimationOptions.Schedule.GAME_TIME` (via `Timeline.schedule`) advances world time between frames while holding each for one tick of wall clock - a day in 3.2 s rather than 20 real minutes. `deriveTimeline` also probes the item's tree (`ItemModelNode.timeDispatchSteps`) to set the cadence from its own table; that search must walk **all** branches, since the clock's dispatch sits behind a `context_dimension` select no offline context can evaluate.
- Uniform tick sampling covers **60 of 64** faces (4 repeats, 4 skips). That is the eased curve - the sun lingers near noon and midnight - not a defect.
- **`context_dimension` is pinned to the overworld** (`ItemModelContext.DIMENSION_OVERWORLD`), the one property besides `display_context` answered outright rather than left unevaluable. `clock.json` is the only one of 1506 vanilla item trees that selects on it, and leaving it unevaluable was not the neutral choice it looked like: the tree would degrade to its **fallback**, which is not a degradation branch but the **Nether/End** one, where vanilla clocks spin randomly (`source: random`, against the overworld case's `source: daytime`). Pinning it keeps the branch matched to the daytime input this renderer computes. It is a fixed answer, not a record component, so `gui()` / `isNeutral()` are untouched.
  Byte-neutral when landed: the branches' 65 entries are identical entry-for-entry at the same `scale: 64.0`, differing only in `source` - which is not parsed at all (`ItemModelNodeDeserializer` reads `property`/`scale`/`target`/`index`/`entries`/`fallback`). Both the render sweep and `parityDump` were unmoved, the latter mattering because `ItemIndexBuilder.addDispatchOnlyItems` resolves trees at pipeline time. **If `source` is ever modelled, this pin is what keeps the clock working** - without it the renderer would honour `random` and resolve a different face per render.
- LOOK gate: `./gradlew itemDayCycle` - clock must sweep noon -> dusk (sun right) -> midnight (moon centred) -> dawn (sun left); compass and sword controls must stay at **one distinct frame** (a compass is a bearing from its holder, never time-driven).

## Parity / vanilla-reference-harness

The sibling [vanilla-reference-harness] drives the actual MC client to render every block + every entity (with variants) at a locked iso pose. Those PNGs are the byte-stable ground truth that `TestEntityParityVanilla` diffs the Java pipeline against. **Harness internals (mixins, bounds walker, state-extraction patterns) live in [vanilla-reference-harness/CLAUDE.md].** This section is the asset-renderer-side knowledge.

### Two pipelines
1. **Java** (vanilla model classes via ASM bytecode walk) - `entity_models.json` + `entity_geometry.json` from the `entityModels` task, consumed at runtime by `EntityRenderer` via `pipeline/loader/EntityModelLoader`.
2. **Vanilla reference** - drives real MC client via the harness; output at `cache/asset-renderer/vanilla/26.1/references/{blocks,entities}/`. **Ground truth.**

### A block icon is the item model at the identity model state - the blockstate plays no part
Vanilla resolves a block item's inventory icon through the item model its `minecraft:item_model` component names (`ItemModelResolver.appendItemLayers` -> `ModelManager.getItemModel`), and for a plain `minecraft:model` root `CuboidItemModelWrapper$Unbaked.bake` bakes it with `BlockModelRotation.IDENTITY`. `BlockStateModelSet.get` has four callers and `BlockModelSet.get` one, **none of them a GUI or item class**, so an icon carries **no variant model, no variant rotation, no `uvlock` and no multipart assembly**. `BlockRenderer.Isometric3D` renders `Block#model()` with a null variant whenever the caller names no state and `Block#modelIcon` is set; a caller-named state gets the full blockstate treatment.
- **`Block#modelIcon` is the whole gate, and it is a population the loader already computed.** It is true exactly when `ItemModelTreeLoader.deriveBlockItemModels` has an entry - a plain `minecraft:model` root naming a block model, **531 of 971 blocks** - which is also the model `BlockIndexBuilder` stamps onto `Block#model()`. The other 440 (doors, wall torches, comparators, levers, cocoa, every block-entity) have a flat sprite or a `SpecialModelRenderer` as their vanilla icon, so the 3D render is this pipeline's own stand-in with no vanilla pose to reproduce, and it keeps the default state's orientation.
- **The rotation is only the visible third of it.** `140` blocks carry a rotated default variant (83 at `y:270` - 58 stairs, 21 doors, 4 wall torches; 38 at `y:180`; 17 at `x:90` - 14 buttons, barrel, grindstone, lever; 2 at `y:90`), but dropping only the rotation still left buttons and pistons wrong, because the variant was also choosing the **model** (`block/oak_button` against the item's `block/oak_button_inventory`; `block/mud_bricks_north_west_mirrored` against the plain cube), and multipart blocks still assembled (`oak_fence`'s post-and-four against the item's `block/oak_fence_inventory` post-and-two). One branch covers all three.
- **A stair is the probe because its two errors cancel exactly.** `block/stairs.json` authors `display.gui` `[30, 135, 0]` where `block/block.json` is `[30, 225, 0]`, and the default state's `y: 270` is `R_y(+90)` (`Quadrant.R270.rotationY` -> `OctahedralGroup.BLOCK_ROT_Y_270` = `ROT_90_Y_POS`). `135 + 90 = 225`, so the authored override was cancelled back to the generic pose and the riser - the model's lone `cullface`-less west face - became a back face and was not drawn at all. The reading that the author baked the `+90` in deliberately is falsified by `block/fence_inventory.json` and `block/wall_inventory.json`, which carry the same `[30, 135, 0]` and appear in no blockstate.
- **The harness takes the same split, from the same shipped file.** `BlockIconGeometry` applies the identical predicate to `items/<name>.json` and reflects the baked `QuadCollection` off the item model vanilla already built, so the reference carries vanilla's own bake. The predicate is deliberately not a runtime proxy (`gui_light`, quad shape), so the two repos cannot drift on which blocks are icons. **`BlockEntityFrameRenderer.submitRawBlockEntity` takes it too**, on its static block half, and the block entity still submits on top - a block entity does not stop a block from having an icon vanilla bakes from a block model. Of the 173 blocks that route to that renderer, 118 take an icon-composition branch and never reach the swap, 55 reach it and 22 of those have a block-model icon; only the 12 shelves and `structure_block` resolve a different model there, the other 9 (beacon, `enchanting_table`, lectern, spawner, `trial_spawner`, vault, `test_instance_block`, `suspicious_sand`, `suspicious_gravel`) name the same model either way.

### Entity model form (`entity_models.json`)
`entity_models.json` is the **normalized model form**: one entry per base entity (`minecraft:wolf`, not a row per colour), keyed under a top-level `models` map - 90 models in 26.1. Each model carries `geometry_ref`/overlays once, plus:
- **`axes`** - orthogonal dimensions, ALL option-encoded in 26.1. `variant` (colour coat, tropical-fish shape) is carried by the 14 models that have it, `state` (wolf wild/tame/angry) and `age` (adult/baby - `age.baby.geometry_ref` points at the dedicated `Baby<X>Model` mesh) by the models that need them. `size` carries a second body mesh the renderer picks between at submit - pufferfish's three puff states, salmon's three scales, and **the armour stand's small form**, which is the full-size mesh under `HumanoidModel.BABY_TRANSFORMER`; its default is the option-less domain member, taken **last-first** so a one-option axis answers `large` rather than `medium`. Option axes are **NOT** id-encoded - they resolve at render from `EntityAppearance` (via `Entity.resolve`).
- **Id-encoding is a dead branch, not a live shape.** The tooling HARDCODES `id_encoded: false` (`EntityVariantAxisResolver:114,272`), so all 14 variant axes ship `false` and the loaded `entityIndex` is exactly **90 rows keyed by plain entity id**: no `minecraft:<id>_<opt>` pseudo-ids are ever synthesised. `variant_of` appears **zero times** in the JSON - it is in-memory only (`EntityIndexBuilder.groupMembership` builds the map at load), never a key on disk. The `idEncoded` branch survives in `EntityIndexBuilder.readDefinition`/`groupMembership` but is unreachable for tooling-generated data.
- **`layers`** - conditional overlays (collar: `tint_by: collar_color`, an option-sourced tint) plus the worn-armour row (`id: "armor"`, see below).
- **A `bones.toggles` entry's `default` is the bones' visibility on a *spawned* entity, and it is derived rather than assumed false.** Vanilla packs several booleans into one synched byte, and a packed flag may store the negative, so one zero byte reads both ways: an armour stand's `showArms()` is `(flags & 4) != 0` (plain bit, arms off) while its `showBasePlate()` is `(flags & 8) == 0` (inverted - the stored bit means "no base plate", so the plate is **on**, and vanilla's own save key spells it `NoBasePlate`). `EntitySpawnFlagResolver` reads it in three hops - `extractRenderState` names the entity accessor a render-state field is filled from, the accessor's `iand` mask and branch give the polarity, `defineSynchedData` gives the byte's registered default - and reads both branch arms off the jump rather than inferring them from its opcode. Anything that does not match answers `false`, which is what an unpacked flag reads as, so `base_plate` is the corpus's only `default: true` of this kind. Assuming false instead put `base_plate` in `hidden` and cost a plate vanilla always draws; it went unnoticed only because the reference set was wrong the same way.
- **Every `<init>` feeds the field-to-bone map, not the first.** `EntityBoneResolver.scanModelHierarchy` walks the model chain, and a model offering both a `(root)` and a `(root, Function)` form builds its parts in the wider one and delegates from the narrower. Reading only the first declared left the whole humanoid family's inherited fields unmapped, so `armor_stand` - the only subject whose `hidden` / `toggles` name one - emitted `leftArm` / `rightArm` against a geometry spelling `left_arm` / `right_arm`, and both the strip and the toggle silently resolved to nothing. A field the map still misses falls back to `StringUtil.toSnakeCase`, the convention vanilla authors bones under; a raw Java field name is never a bone name.
- **There is no badge-less job villager, so `VillagerLevel` has no `NONE`.** `VillagerData`'s constructor raises any level to one and `VillagerProfessionLayer.submit` clamps again into `LEVEL_LOCATIONS`' five, so the `texture_by: profession_level` pass draws for every real job. `EntityAppearance.villagerLevel` is an `Optional<VillagerLevel>` (unset resolves to `VillagerLevel.minimum()`, ordinal 0); what suppresses a badge is `VillagerProfession.drawsBadge()` (`NONE` / `NITWIT`) and `isBaby`, both answered before a level is read. Modelling the unset level as "no badge" cost **28 rows** at 0.81 - 2.38 across villager and `zombie_villager`; the tell was that the reference naming `villager_level=stone` is **byte-identical** to the profession-only one it pairs with.
- Per-variant option `textures` hold `{wild, tame, angry, baby}` texture refs; `group_of` carries cross-entity groupings (mooshroom -> cow).
- **A variant option may name a `block`**, and that is the mooshroom's mushrooms. Vanilla holds a `BlockState` on the `$Variant` constant (`RED -> Blocks.RED_MUSHROOM`, `BROWN -> Blocks.BROWN_MUSHROOM`) and `MushroomCowRenderer.extractRenderState` re-bakes one block model per variant for the layer to submit at three poses - so the block is a property of the coat, and it rides the coat. `VariantBlockTable` is the one walk of that `<clinit>`; the `block_overlays[]` row carries the `DEFAULT` alias's block and a coat that wraps a different one carries its own (**omit the identity** - only `brown` writes a key). `EntityIndexBuilder.buildVariantRow` folds it in at load through `BlockOverlayLayer.withBlockId`, on **fixed rows only** - a selectable row's block is the caller's held one.
- **The canvas is sized from the DEFAULT coat's block, never the selected one** (`EntityRenderer.boundsBlockOverlays`). Vanilla's family-fit pre-pass builds a fresh render state, so its variant is the enum's default and it never sees the coat being drawn. Measured, not assumed: vanilla renders both mooshroom coats into one `388x564` frame, but the brown one's content is **14 px taller** (top margin 16 against 2, bottom margin 0 on both) - the frame is sized once and the taller coat reaches further up inside it. Sizing per coat instead gave every mooshroom row a 578-tall canvas and shifted all three off the reference. The coat union in `unionVariantSilhouettes` is what keeps a **baby** mooshroom on the family canvas, since a baby's own resolved definition drops block overlays entirely.
- **A `tint_by` axis is NOT "multiply by the selected dye"** - the axis decides what colour a dye draws as, via `TintAxis.resolve(DyeColor)`. Four of the five axes take `DyeColor.argb()` (the vanilla `textureDiffuseColor`, verified against an ASM walk of `DyeColor.<clinit>`); `WOOL` alone takes `DyeColor.woolArgb()`, because vanilla fills the sheep's render state from `ColorLerper.Type.SHEEP` = `getModifiedColor(dye, 0.75f)` - every channel floored at three quarters, **except WHITE, replaced outright by `0xFFE6E6E6`**. `Vanilla` derives that column in a constructor rather than transcribing it, WHITE being the one full override. A wool render reading the plain dye is a third too bright on all sixteen dyes. Adding a dye axis is one enum constant plus its `tint_by` emission; adding a *non-identity* one is a `resolve` override, never a branch in the renderer. **The tooling emitting a resolved per-dye table instead was built and withdrawn - see the ledger.**
- **`when: {tinted: true}` is a lossy transcription of a dye comparison.** The tooling emits it on seeing `GETFIELD dye; GETSTATIC DyeColor.<X>; IF_ACMP` in the layer's `submit` (vanilla's `woolColor == WHITE -> return`) and discards *which* dye. `AppearanceGate.TintedGate` therefore fires when the selection resolves to something other than the row's **own baked tint** - which is what that dye resolves to - rather than merely "a dye is selected". Reading it the loose way draws an undercoat on a white sheep that vanilla omits. The render path evaluates the gate itself; there is deliberately no second copy of the predicate beside it.
- **An overlay's `tint` is whatever colour vanilla submits it with**, resolved by `EntityOverlayResolver.extractOverlayTint`: the render-supplied source first (a state getter chasing `ColorLerper.getColor` at its WHITE default - the sheep wool; a state int chasing `DyeColor.getTextureDiffuseColor()` - the fish pattern), then a **constant literal**, read as the value standing directly after the `OverlayTexture.NO_OVERLAY` argument the colour follows in `submitModel`'s signature. That literal arm is how the two energy-swirl rows carry `0xFF808080`: vanilla's aura is half strength because its **vertex colour** is grey at full alpha (`entity.fsh` does `color *= vertexColor` before `BlendFunction.ADDITIVE`'s `src=ONE dst=ONE`), NOT because of any alpha - at `srcAlpha=ONE` the alpha channel could not have dimmed it. The walk climbs the super chain (the constant sits on `EnergySwirlLayer`, the roster names its subclasses); a white literal is the no-tint identity. Exactly 3 sites in the 316 `client/renderer/entity` classes push a constant there, and only this one is non-white.
- **An overlay's `blend` is a *composition*, and `cutout` is the absence of a blend rather than a kind of one.** `dev.simplified.image.pixel.BlendMode` carries the three this pipeline uses - `NORMAL` (source-over), `ADD` (the energy swirl) and `REPLACE` (Porter-Duff source: write the fragment over the destination, alpha included, and never read it). `EntityPipelineTraits.blendTokenOf` emits `cutout` when a layer's **resolved** pipeline declares no `BlendFunction` at all, which is what vanilla's cutout pipelines do; `EntityIndexBuilder.parseBlend` maps it to `REPLACE`, and `translucent` / `normal` both stay `NORMAL` because source-over is right for both. **`REPLACE` and `NORMAL` differ only where a fragment's alpha is strictly between 0 and 255** - `ColorMath.blendNormal` short-circuits `if (srcAlpha == 0xFF) return src`, a 0-alpha texel is discarded before the composite, the `MULTIPLY` tint packs the destination's (the texel's) alpha and `Shading.apply` passes alpha through - so declaring a pass `cutout` is free on any geometry whose texels are all one or the other. That is an identity between the two composites, not a property of a texture set, and it is the reason 7 overlays carry the token while exactly one row's pixels moved. **`REPLACE` lives in the `image` library rather than here.** It arrived as an asset-renderer-local `engine.raster.Composition` so a measured render fix (`tropical_fish~pattern=brinely` `6.4738 -> 0.8791`) was not gated behind the cross-repo publish cycle, and was promoted once the shape was proven: Porter-Duff source is a standard compositing operator, so the enum is where it belongs, and one enum means the illegal state (a replace set *and* a blend chosen) is not representable. `Composition.composite` collapsed into `ColorMath.blend`, `ADDITIVE` became `ADD`, and the promotion was byte-neutral across all five sweeps. The one visible trace is the diagnostic: `PipelineParityDump` emits `overlay.blend().name()`, so an additive overlay now dumps `ADD` where it dumped `ADDITIVE` - dump labels either side of this change are not comparable for that one field.
- **A block an entity holds is tinted at the no-world-context point, never at a biome.** `CarriedBlockLayer` submits through `BlockModelRenderState.submit`, whose signature carries no level and no `BlockPos`; the tint was baked earlier by `BlockModelResolver.update` -> `BlockStateModelWrapper.updateTints` -> `BlockTintSource.color(BlockState)`, the level-free arm, which for grass is `GrassColor.getDefaultColor() = get(0.5, 1.0)` - the grass colormap centre `(127, 127)`. `colorInWorld` -> `BiomeColors.getAverageGrassColor` sits on the other arm and this path never reaches it, so the biome the entity stands in cannot affect the block it carries. That is the same point the block-item icon resolves at, so **one constant serves both**: `Biome.INVENTORY_DEFAULT`, which also carries the fixed `FoliageColor` / `DryFoliageColor` defaults those two targets take instead of a colormap sample at all. `assets/minecraft/items/grass_block.json` states it as shipped data (`"tints": [{"type": "minecraft:grass", "downfall": 1.0, "temperature": 0.5}]`), so it is checkable without a bytecode walk. Sampling `PLAINS` (`0.8 / 0.4`) instead is a **hue** error - R x1.17, B x0.83, G unmoved - which mean signed luma cancels to about `-2` while mean `|delta|` stays near `18`; read a suspected tint fault per channel, never through luma.
- **The carried-block overlay path applies blockstate variant rotation, and the icon path must not. This is the mirror image of the icon rule below, not the same decision.** A carried block is an in-world block: vanilla reaches it through `CarriedBlockLayer` -> `BlockModelResolver` -> `BlockModelSet.get(BlockState)`, the one caller of that method. That cache is pre-seeded **only** from `BuiltInBlockModels` (157 named block-entity/special blocks), so an ordinary block **misses** and falls through to `createFallbackModel` -> `BlockStateModelSet.get(state)` - it really does resolve a blockstate, and a blockstate model carries its variant's rotation baked in. An icon reaches `ModelManager.getItemModel` and never touches a blockstate at all. `EntityRenderer.buildBlockOverlayTriangles` appends the rotation **after** its `translate(+0.5)`, so it is rightmost and applies first, to the still-origin-centred cube - the same centre vanilla bakes about (`FaceBakery.bakeVertex` -> `rotateVertexBy(v, BLOCK_MIDDLE = (0.5, 0.5, 0.5))`). Both angles are negated, as in `BlockRenderer.buildVariantRotation`.
- **A block drawn with no world position behind it draws a FIXED entry of its weighted variant list, and that is a real branch rather than a tie-break.** Vanilla picks between an authored variants array at random seeded by the block's position; a held or carried block has no position, so `BlockModelResolver.update` seeds with a hardcoded `42`. `RandomSource.create` is `LegacyRandomSource` = `java.util.Random` and `BitRandomSource.nextInt(int)` is `Random`'s to the branch, so the draw is reproducible arithmetic: for the three array lengths 26.1 ships it lands on index `1` of 2, **`2` of 4**, and `11` of 16. A carried `grass_block` therefore draws `{"model": "block/grass_block", "y": 180}`, not the authored first - which is the whole of `enderman~carried=grass_block`'s former `1.6551`. **34 blocks author such an array**, 10 of the 56 arrays vary the *model* across entries (`stone`/`stone_mirrored`, deepslate, bedrock, sculk) and not merely the rotation, and **none carries an explicit weight**, so `nextInt(size)` is the index rather than a cumulative walk - a weighted array would need one and would silently draw wrong without it.
  - **It is resolved at index build, and that is the only place it can be.** The array is *shipped data*, merged across the whole pack stack (`BlockStateLoader.load` walks `stack.ascending()` honouring `hides`), so a resource pack can change its length, models and rotations. Baking the draw into a tooling table the way `block_defaults.json` bakes `registerDefaultState` would be right for vanilla and **silently** wrong for any pack that edits that blockstate - the draw is `nextInt(size)`, so one added entry moves the answer for every entry. `block_defaults` is safe to freeze precisely because bytecode is not pack-overridable; a variants array is. So `BlockStateLoader` keeps the array raw on `ApplyDto.weighted`, `BlockIndexBuilder.drawWithoutPosition` draws once after the merge, and `Block.Variant.noPosition` carries the result. Nothing draws on the render path.
  - **It cannot ride `entity_models.json`.** The enderman's `block_overlays[]` row is `selectable: true` with **no `block` key** - the id comes from the caller (`EntityAppearance.carried` -> `withBlockId`), so at tooling time there is no block and no rotation to walk, and an op on that row would apply to every block it carries. The three rows that name a fixed block (mooshroom, iron_golem, snow_golem) are exactly the ones needing no rotation.
- **A layer may reach its render type only through a static helper.** `EntityPipelineTraits.collectFactoryCalls` descends into the statics a layer's instance body **actually invokes**, two hops deep, resolving each the way `invokestatic` does - javac names the calling class rather than the declaring one, so the lookup must walk the super chain. Two hops is the floor: `RenderLayer.coloredCutoutModelCopyLayerRender` delegates to `renderColoredCutoutModel` before reaching `RenderTypes.entityCutout`. Accepting *any* static in the hierarchy instead would credit every subclass with the base's inherited helpers, which is why the walk follows calls rather than declarations. A target the extracted jar does not hold ends the branch.

`pipeline/loader/EntityModelLoader` is a thin orchestrator: two pure `document.as` reads - `entity_geometry.json` -> `Map<String, EntityModelData>` and `entity_models.json` -> the raw `pipeline/index/RawEntityModelsFile` DTO tree - handed to `pipeline/index/EntityIndexBuilder.assemble`, which expands the model form into the flat `Map<String, Entity>` (asset.`Entity`) the renderer consumes. The geometry join, the mesh surgery (hidden-bone strip, `retainExactParts` subset, `grow` inflate + the auto-emitted `0.001` depth-clearance bump), the axes pivot, the per-variant sub-`Entity` fold, and the cross-entity grouping all live in `EntityIndexBuilder`; the leaf decodes (hex tint, `blend` token, texture strip, transform ops) happen there too, where the `Diagnostics` handle is available. `Entity` carries `Entity.Axes` (`stateTextures` / `babyModel` / `largeShape` / `sizeModels` / `sizeScales` / `variants`) + `Entity.Layers` (`collar` / `equipment` / `markings` / `humanoidArmor`), plus `withoutBlockOverlays()` and `resolve(EntityAppearance)` (the render-time axis fold). `EntityAppearance` carries the `variant` / `state` / `carried` / `age` / collar-colour / sheared / `size` / `pattern` render selections. **Baby renders draw the baby overlay form and skip block-overlays** (adult overlay geometry would render adult-sized around the smaller baby body). An overlay contributes to a baby only when it declares a `baby` sub-node - the two villagers' biome robe, the trader llama's baby caparison (its own `0.2` inflate + `trader_llama_baby` texture, against the adult's `0.5`), the drowned's outer shell and the sheep's wool; an overlay with no baby form draws nothing on the baby. **The `baby` node may name its own `geometry`**, and that is not cosmetic: a baby pass is only sometimes the baby body. Vanilla registers `SHEEP_BABY_WOOL` against the very `LayerDefinition` slot `SHEEP_BABY` uses, so the sheep's delta needs none and stays coincident with the body it dresses; `DROWNED_BABY_OUTER_LAYER` is its own definition, and `BabyZombieModel#createBodyLayer` hardcodes two of its head cubes' deformations instead of driving them off its parameter, so no inflate of the baby body reaches that shell and the delta names `BabyDrownedModel#createBodyLayer@grow=0.25`. A delta naming its own mesh does NOT inherit the row's `grow` - the tooling already baked that mesh's deformation, and inheriting would apply the adult inflate a second time on top. Baby texture source chain: variant-option `baby_texture` -> renderer `isBaby` binding -> `<adult>_baby` naming convention. Schema contract + wolf example in gitignored `notes/entity-family-schema.md` + `notes/sample_model.json`.

### Worn armour (a `layers[]` row, no sibling file)
Vanilla never derives worn armour from the wearer's own mesh - `LayerDefinitions` builds a handful of `ArmorModelSet`s and hands each renderer the one its subject wears, so a skeleton's narrow limbs and a giant's scaled-up body dress in the same boxes. **There is no armour file.** The shell rides the wearer's `layers[]` armour row like any other mesh in the pipeline:
```json
{ "source": "HumanoidArmorLayer", "layer_index": 3, "id": "armor",
  "overlay": { "geometry": "HumanoidModel#createBaseArmorMesh",
               "grow": { "inner": 0.5, "outer": 1.0 } } }
```
`id: "armor"` identifies the row, `overlay.geometry` points into `entity_geometry.json` (so `GeometryRefClosureTest` covers it both ways, forward and reverse, with no special case), and `overlay.grow` carries the set's two layer deformations. A wearer vanilla hands a second, genuinely distinct shell repeats the same members under `overlay.alternate` (see below). **The payload lives in `overlay` because every `layers[]` row's does** - collar (`texture`), markings (`texture_by` / `textures_by_value`), equipment (`geometry` / `layer_type` / `material_assets` / `default_material`) - so `RawLayer` stays a three-member routing shell (`id` / `when` / `overlay`) and armour reuses `RawLayerOverlay.geometry` rather than minting row-level members only it would set. 14 rows over **5 distinct meshes** in 26.1: `HumanoidModel#createBaseArmorMesh`, `ArmorStandArmorModel#createBaseMesh`, `ZombieVillagerModel#createBaseArmorMesh`, the baby `HumanoidModel#createBabyArmorMesh` (two geometry entries, one per bound arm pose) and the small armour stand's `ArmorStandArmorModel#createBaseMesh@baby=HumanoidModel.BABY_TRANSFORMER`. The generic adult deformations are `0.5` / `1.0`; the outer `1.02` is the **piglin family's alone** (3 of the 14 rows); the generic baby's are the anisotropic `(-0.1, 0.3, 0.3)` / `(-0.1, 0.5, 0.3)` and the piglin baby's a uniform `0.7`.
- **A second shell names the selection that reaches it, because vanilla reaches both kinds through one flag and this pipeline through two axes.** `overlay.alternate` carries `when` plus a `form`: six wearers write `{"age": "baby"}` / `"form": "baby"`, and the small armour stand writes `{"size": "small"}` / `"form": "adult"`. Both are read rather than inferred - the **axis** from the single name token the second `ModelLayers` field carries that the first does not (`ZOMBIE_ARMOR` against `ZOMBIE_BABY_ARMOR`, `ARMOR_STAND_ARMOR` against `ARMOR_STAND_SMALL_ARMOR`), the **form** from the `*_ARMOR_PARTS_PER_SLOT` constant the set factory hands its fan-out. They do not follow from one another: the stand's shell is aged-down geometry built by the *adult* factory, so it keeps the adult parts, sheets and trim. `Shell.forAppearance` evaluates the gate once in `Entity.resolve`, **outside** the age fork, which is what lets one slot serve two axes and matches vanilla picking the set off the flag alone rather than off the body mesh.
- **A bone's uniform `scale` is honoured by multiplying each operand, never the assembled corners.** Every shell vanilla registers untransformed leaves the scale at 1, so the whole thing was latent until the small stand's shell arrived carrying one per bone; drawing it unscaled puts a full-size helmet on a half-size body. Per-operand keeps `x * 1f == x`, so the identity is free - which is why `ShellWalk.of` pre-multiplies a row's operands and `BoneKit.scaledCubeBounds` scales origin, size and grow separately rather than scaling a finished box.
- **Every cube box in the renderer is formed in vanilla's operand order, and `Box.grown` is the one place it happens.** `javap` of `ModelPart$Cube`'s constructor is the authority: it computes `f = x + sizeX` from the **un-grown** origin, then `x -= growX`, then `f += growX`, so a cube's corners are `origin - grow` and `(origin + size) + grow`. `ShellPart.Mesh.boxFor` used to compute `((origin - grow) + size) + grow + grow` - algebraically the same, arithmetically not - while the bounds side already had vanilla's order. **The `+0.0706` once recorded for closing that gap was stale and an overestimate**, measured before the operands moved onto `ShellPart.Mesh` pre-scaled. Re-measured and landed, the price is `60.0043 -> 60.0047` (**+0.0004**, 0.0007%) over 8 rows - five better, three worse, `zombie_villager` the largest at `+0.0068` because its shell carries the most non-zero cube deformations - plus `+0.0371` on one armour-diagnostic row, with block / item / player unchanged to the digit and the player CRC pins untouched (the player dresses `ShellPart.Body`, never `Mesh`). **Do not read those wobbles as evidence about which form is right**: `+-0.002` on a `0.25` row is inside the sub-texel float floor, so the bytecode is the arbiter and the sum is not.
- **The two corner ASSEMBLIES still cannot be unified in either direction - sharing the growth expression is not sharing the walk.** Measuring `screenBounds` from `shell.walk().parts()` and projecting each `row.boxFor(slot)`'s corners gives up the alpha-tight per-face walk, since a row carries no bone-chain matrix and its anchor is a leaf-first pivot sum where that walk composes matrices. Projecting a row's eight corners **is** `computeScreenBounds`'s own `texture == null` branch, so it was priced without writing a second walk: entity `60.0043 -> 413.4277`, with **12 of the 14 armoured rows moving from a maximum of `0.7232` to a minimum of `27.0666`** (`giant` `0.7232 -> 35.3224`), and armour `18.2100 -> 19.7463`. What moves is the **canvas** - a re-sample of every pixel on those rows rather than a sub-pixel drift. The two outputs are also **180 degrees about X apart**: the triangle side ends in the upright frame (`intoUprightFrameBounds`) while `screenBounds` measures the Y-down mesh, so handing the bounds walk an assembled box would measure the shell in a frame reflected in Y and Z from the body's own bounds - growing the canvas downward for a helmet and upward for boots. **This is the change most likely to be re-proposed; it is refused by measurement now as well as by mechanism.** What *is* shared is `Shell.walk()` (`ShellWalk`): the per-slot coverage and the per-bone leaf-first summed anchor, resolved once when the shell is indexed rather than once per equipped slot per render in each of the two consumers. The anchor keeps leaf-first order because float addition does not associate. The walk answers in **two shapes because its two consumers iterate differently** - `ShellPart.Mesh` rows for the triangle builder, one per cube in the mesh's own bone-then-cube order, and a per-slot bone-name set for the bounds adapter, which rebuilds a bone tree. They agree by construction (a bone with cubes has a row exactly when it is covered; a bone without cubes contributes an empty cube list either way), so this is one walk indexed twice rather than two walks. **The row order is the load-bearing part and it is still the mesh's own** - it decides a coplanar tie and keeps a part adjacent to the overlay box parented to it - which is why it is a `List`; the coverage set is lookup-only, so neither an immutable map's per-run salt nor a mutable one's insertion order can reach a render.
- **`ArmorKit.slotMesh`'s tree rebuild is the adapter between those two assemblies rather than a redundancy, and it stays.** It restates "these bones, grown by this much, root seated" in the vocabulary `EntityGeometryKit.computeScreenBounds` accepts, because that walk composes full `BoneKit` chain matrices where a row's own anchor sums pivots. Deleting it means giving `computeScreenBounds` a second input shape - a kept-bone set, an extra deformation and a root offset - and threading that root offset into `buildChainTransforms`, both of which the entity's own bounds path shares, to save four `EntityModelData` copies per armoured render on a rebuild measured at under 1% of runtime. Copying the tree is the cheap way to share the inputs without sharing an assembly.
- **The mesh is registered ungrown, and the two deformations travel on the row.** `ArmorMeshIndex` walks `createRoots` for the registrations (set field, base mesh factory behind the set factory's first `INVOKEDYNAMIC`, and the call site's two `CubeDeformation`s) and `EntityLayersResolver.armorRow` registers a plain `GeometryRequest.overlay(..., 64, 32, NO_GROW)` in the shared manifest. Each cube then carries only its own `CubeDeformation.extend` (`hat +0.5`, legs `-0.1`, zombie-villager `body`/legs `+0.1`), and `ShellWalk.of` sums `deformation + cube.grow` once when the shell is indexed - the same two operands in the same order the parser used, so it is bit-identical to baking it - leaving the render to pick between the two pre-summed growths. That collapse is what makes the piglin family (identical to the generic set bar its outer `1.02`) **share** the generic geometry entry instead of duplicating it.
- **Selection is data**: `EntityLayersResolver.resolveArmorMesh` resolves the `ArmorModelSet` field name leaf-first along the renderer's constructor chain, falling back to the armour set among the registration lambda's `ModelLayers` references (the piglin family takes its set as a constructor argument, so no field along the chain names it). All 14 armoured entities resolve one. The name is a lookup key into `ArmorMeshIndex` only - it never ships.
- **Being armoured IS carrying a resolved shell.** `Entity.Layers.humanoidArmor` is one `Optional<Shell>` (mesh + `innerGrow` + `outerGrow` + `meshScale` + `form` + the optional second shell + the walk); there is no separate classification flag a wearer could hold while its mesh failed to resolve. A walk failure therefore drops that wearer off `HumanoidArmorRosterTest`'s pinned 14 and fails loudly instead of falling back to a shared mesh. `forAppearance` answers with the wearer's own second shell or, for the seven without one, with itself - which is vanilla's own way of saying a wearer has only the one.
- **The shell is a top-level type in `asset.equipment`, and what dresses a wearer asks whichever of three types owns each answer.** `Shell` owns what varies by *shell* - `mesh`, the two deformations, `meshScale` / `meshOffset`, `walk`, the `form` it names and the second shell an appearance selects; `ArmorForm` owns what varies by that form's *shape*; `ArmorSlot` owns what varies by *slot* alone, and it now names no `lib.minecraft.renderer` type at all. **The delegations `Shell` used to carry are gone** - `sheet(slot)`, `trimLayer(slot)` and `grow(slot)` were one-line forwards onto the other two, and a forward is a second place to read one answer rather than a second answer. **`ArmorForm` is not folded into `Shell`, and what it survives on is its two part tables**: two shells of one shape share those tables and their sheet answer by reference, and the player's own armour path never holds a `Shell` at all - it dresses its own six body boxes in `ArmorForm.ADULT`'s answers - so inlining would both duplicate shipped data per instance and force static stand-ins for the enum constant it deleted.
- **There is one 3D armour build, and the three arguments it varies by are the whole of the difference between a player and a worn shell.** `ArmorKit.buildArmor3D` takes a flat `ShellPart` row list, a `UnaryOperator<Box>` frame mapping and an `ArmorForm`: the player hands its own body boxes, `identity()` and `ADULT`; a wearer hands its shell's cubes, the crossing into the render frame, and its shell's own form. **The frame crossing is the only one of the three that is arithmetic**, which is why it is the argument rather than the branch - the mesh arm's `toRenderFrame` + `intoUprightFrameBounds` + `Box.of` runs on the same values in the same order whether it is called from a loop or through the mapping, while a branch would have invited the two arms to drift. Nothing downstream of that entry point branches on which wearer it is serving.
- **Two bones in the armour corpus are shipped oddities rather than defects to normalise, and both are vanilla's.** `inner_body` ships on **both** baby shells, is named by no slot and is not a descendant of one, so `covers` answers false in all four slots and it can never draw - and its cube is byte-identical to `body`'s (origin `[-3,-3,-1.5]`, size `[6,5,3]`, uv `[0,17]`, pivot `[0,18,0]`, no grow). And a baby shell's feet are **cross-parented**: `right_foot.parent == "left_leg"` and `left_foot.parent == "right_leg"`, confirmed by `javap` of `HumanoidModel.createBabyArmorMesh` rather than inferred from the emitted JSON (`FrogModel#createBodyLayer`, by contrast, parents `right_foot` under `right_leg`). `ShellWalk` follows the crossed edge, so each baby foot takes the opposite leg's pivot `[-+1.5, 20.0, 0.5]`. Pairing the sides up would be editing shipped data.
- **The armour mesh bypasses the entity mesh surgery.** `EntityIndexBuilder.humanoidArmorOf` joins `geometries.get(coord)` raw - no hidden-bone strip, no `retainExactParts` subset, no auto-emitted `0.001` depth-clearance bump. The shell is a shared set vanilla hands the wearer, not a derivative of the wearer's own mesh.
- **The whole-mesh `MeshTransformer.scaling`** three sets are registered through (giant `6.0`, husk `1.0625`, wither skeleton `1.2`) rides the **armour row**, not the geometry: `ArmorMeshIndex` latches the factor at `MeshTransformer.scaling` and stamps it onto the **copy** `ArmorModelSet.map` makes (never the shared generic operand the eleven unscaled wearers also load), `EntityLayersResolver.armorRow` emits `overlay.scaled` when it is not `1`, and `Shell` carries `meshScale` with `meshOffset()` **derived** as `24.016f * (1f - meshScale)` - vanilla's feet anchor, in the same expression and precision the tooling baked the wearers' own pivots with, so the two agree bit for bit. The mesh stays ungrown *and* unscaled, so the four sets over the generic mesh still share one geometry entry. **`EntityArmorFrame.of` is deleted** - recovering the transform from the wearer's `body` bone was exact only because 11 of the 14 wearers are pose-neutral and the other 3 carry exactly that anchor, it had no rotation member (`AdultFelineModel`'s body pose has one), and it was already wrong for a baby, whose own body pivot is `[0,17.5..18.75,0]` rather than a mesh transform. Landing it moved 0 of 401 rows.
- **The shell is built two-sided, and that is a coverage contract before it is a lighting one.** Vanilla submits worn armour through `ARMOR_CUTOUT_NO_CULL` - `withCull(false)` alongside `ALPHA_CUTOUT 0.1` and `PER_FACE_LIGHTING` (`RenderPipelines.<clinit>`) - so where a box's near face is cut away by a transparent texel, the **far face of that same box shows through the hole**. Building armour with the block cuboid's `cullBackFaces=true` dropped those far faces whole, costing each of the 14 wearers 2-5 entire cube faces wherever the shell stands clear of the body behind it: a strict-subset silhouette (`java-only=0`, `vanilla-only` 121-1051 px) reading as flat diamond wedges at the helmet corner and both boots. `SurfaceTraits.WORN_SHELL` is what carries it, against `SurfaceTraits.OPAQUE_BODY`'s `true`, so block/item/portal/player-skin geometry cannot inherit it. **Armour geometry does not differ from block geometry by a code path - it differs by those two bits**, which is why one `buildBox` serves the block's unit cube, the item slab, the player's body boxes, the cape and the shell, and why the distinction is two named constants rather than two entry points. The same flag then selects the per-face lighting form in `Lighting.EntityLighting.shade` - a face seen from behind is lit by its camera-facing orientation, one seen from the front is bit-identical either way, so **no already-visible pixel changes shade**. The player's own armour takes the cull half only: it is never turned back into the entity frame, so it keeps the cull-blind `Lighting.inventory` shade.
- **The slot is a property of the armour, not of the trim system, and its declaration order is a rendering contract.** `ArmorSlot` (`option.spec`) is a top-level enum rather than a member of the trim type - 55 of the 57 main-source lines naming it have nothing to do with trims, and its only payload `key` is read by the item-icon trim overlay alone. It declares LEGGINGS first because that is vanilla armour layer 2, the innermost, so iterating it paints the leggings before the three layer-1 pieces and the chestplate wins on the torso and the boots on the lower legs. **Every consumer honours it now**, the 3D skin path included - it emitted HELMET, CHESTPLATE, LEGGINGS, BOOTS from a table of its own. Closing that divergence is **measured byte-neutral**, not argued: the armoured-FULL CRC pin held across the change on its own, and of the 36 player contact-sheet cells the three single-slot ones without a helmet (`chestplate_only`, `leggings_only`, `boots_only`) did not move. The reason is that path's two inflations (`0.015` outer, `0.008` inner) separate every shared bone, so no equal-depth contest between the layers ever arose to be reordered. **All three armour walks iterate slot-outermost now, and on the 2D path that is what makes the contract unconditional rather than contingent.** Part-outermost painted correctly too, but only because the six body-part canvas rectangles tile without overlap - all fifteen pairs are disjoint, the head at `y 8..16` clear of the torso and arms at `y -4..8` and the legs at `y -16..-4`, with the two arms and two legs at disjoint X. That same disjointness is what made the inversion byte-neutral (60 of 60 contact sheets), and it is exactly why the order should not rest on it: with the slot outermost a later slot paints over an earlier one whatever the rectangles do. **Do not invert it back to save a coverage lookup.**
- **A trim's two halves travel as one value.** `ArmorTrim` is a `record(pattern, color)` on an `Optional` field of `ArmorPiece`, so the half-present state is unrepresentable and no compositor has to ask whether both are there before reading either. The four `isPresent() && isPresent()` guards it replaced were each correct; the point is that no call site could construct the state they guarded against.
- **What a slot answers about itself lives on `ArmorSlot`; what the shell answers lives on `ArmorForm`; and the split is not cosmetic.** Three questions are the slot's alone and are constant-specific overrides on it - which member of a layer-indexed pair it takes (`onLayer`, leggings inner, the other three outer), the inflation it applies to the player's own boxes (`skinInflate`), and whether it covers a named part's **children** (`keepsChildren`, the helmet alone, which is what puts the head's overlay box on a helmet and nothing else) - plus `skinOverlayInflate`, the inflation that second box sits at. **`onLayer` is generic `<T> T onLayer(T, T)` and that is a package edge, not a style.** It is asked about three pairs - a shell's two deformations, one cube's two growths, and the two equipment layers `ArmorForm.layerType` picks between (the last `slot ==` in the tree) - and naming that third pair's type would put `asset.equipment` back on `option.spec`'s import list, which two commits deliberately bought its way off. **Both paths honour it now.** The skin path used to draw one box where the mesh path drew two, and whether the shell's `hat` cube costs visible pixels there was an open question: it does, on **all eight** armour materials, and on nothing without a helmet. The second box reads the part's **overlay** rectangle - the head's is `uv (32, 0)`, which is that very cube's origin on vanilla's own sheet - at `ARMOR_INFLATE * 1.5`, the ratio the cube's own further `+0.5` puts it at over the layer-1 `1.0`. It is a real behaviour change with a real gate: the armoured-FULL CRC moved `0x9DAA524F -> 0x60CFB45A` while the unarmoured `FULL` / `SKULL` pins and all five parity sums held. Everything that varies by *shell* stays on `ArmorForm`, which is why the player skin path now asks `ArmorForm.ADULT.layerType(slot)` and `.trimLayer(slot)` rather than carrying its own `LayerType` and raw-string ternaries: those literals were that form's answers verbatim, so they were a second copy rather than a second rule. **`ArmorForm.playerSlots(part)` is deliberately `static` and ADULT-only** - only 6 of the 12 bone names in the armour corpus have a player body part, a baby shell's `waist` and its two feet have none, and a form-parameterised accessor would let a miss drop a box silently. The hardcode is the guard. It is asked of the **part** rather than of the slot because both readers want it that way: the 2D compositor tests one part against one slot, and the 3D build stamps the answer onto that part's row.
- **One `ShellPart` shape serves both armour builds, and the two arms share the shape and never the arithmetic.** A row answers three questions - which slots draw it, what box it occupies once a slot's deformation is applied, and where it reads its faces from on whichever sheet - so `ArmorKit` has **one** slot loop and **one** box builder where it had two of each. `ShellPart.Mesh` is one cube of a worn shell, carrying its anchored scaled origin, its scaled size and **both** growths a slot picks between (each the shell's layer deformation summed with the cube's own, taken through the bone's scale); `ShellPart.Body` is one of the player's own body boxes, inflated by the scalar the slot is calibrated for. **Neither is the other in different units** - `0.015` is half a Minecraft pixel in the body scopes and a fifth of that in the skull scope - so each arm keeps its own expression. **The mesh arm carries operands rather than a finished box on purpose**: its upper corner is `((origin - g) + size) + g + g`, which does not separate into a box plus an expansion because float addition does not associate.
- **The helmet's second box is a peer row on both paths, not a branch.** On the mesh path it is the shell's own `hat` cube, whose row carries HELMET because `ArmorForm.covers` walked the parent chain at index time. On the player path it is a second `ShellPart.Body` over the same body box reading the part's overlay rectangle, emitted directly after the row it sits over and covered by exactly the slots that keep a named part's children. `keepsChildren()` is read where the rows are built and never at render.
- **Both parent walks are bounded by a visiting set, not a depth cap** - `ArmorForm.covers` and `ShellWalk`'s pivot chain. The real maximum chain is **2 hops** (`hat_rim -> hat -> head`) against the old cap of 8, so cap and set agree on everything shipped; the set is what actually rules out the cycle the cap was standing in for. `ArmorForm` is where the coverage rule belongs because the child rule was already documented on `ArmorForm.parts` while living in the kit.
- Slot pruning, the equipment sheet and whether a trim is drawn all live on **`ArmorForm`** (`asset.equipment`), one constant per shell, because all three are properties of the shell rather than of the wearer. **Whether a trim is drawn is read off the sheet rather than off a flag the form carries**, which is what vanilla's own early return keys on: `EquipmentLayerRenderer.renderLayers` returns before submitting one whenever the *layer* is `HUMANOID_BABY`, so `layerType(slot) == HUMANOID_BABY` is that same answer with one fewer field that could disagree with it - equal over all 2x4 pairs, since `ADULT` never answers `HUMANOID_BABY` and `BABY` always does. `ADULT` mirrors vanilla's `ADULT_ARMOR_PARTS_PER_SLOT` and `BABY` its `BABY_ARMOR_PARTS_PER_SLOT`; a helmet keeps its part **and that part's children**, which is what puts the head's second `hat` box on a helmet and what lets a baby's boots reach the feet parented under its legs, and the other three slots keep exactly the parts they name.
- **Baby sets are indexed too, and a baby wears its own shell.** Vanilla's `createBabyArmorMeshSet(inner, outer, PartPose)` builds a genuinely different mesh - a `waist` the adult shell has no counterpart for, feet parented under the legs, and a 64x64 sheet against the adult's 64x32. Six of the fourteen wearers have one (`drowned`, `husk`, `piglin`, `zombie`, `zombie_villager`, `zombified_piglin`), it rides `overlay.baby` on the armour row, and `Entity.resolve` swaps to it on the age arm so **nothing downstream branches on age**.
  - **A second set is a distinct shell only when it is a distinct shell.** Vanilla says "only the one" by handing the layer one set twice, and the piglin brute passes the same field twice and means nothing by it. `ArmorMeshIndex.Set.sameShellAs` rejects it by construction; matching on the `*_BABY_ARMOR` name would take eight. The small armour stand is **not** rejected: its second set is the adult one re-registered through `HumanoidModel.BABY_TRANSFORMER`, which builds genuinely different boxes, so it compares as the distinct shell it is and rides `overlay.alternate` under `when: {size: small}`.
  - **The pose is a mesh argument, not a layer one.** `createBabyArmorMesh` reads `pose.x()/y()/z()` into the two arm pivots, so the piglin family's `PartPose(0.5, -0.5, 0)` builds a different shell rather than moving the same one. `GeometryRequest.PoseParam` binds the slot, the parser folds the accessors, and the key says so: `HumanoidModel#createBabyArmorMesh@pose=0.5,-0.5,0.0`. Its base factory sits behind a **capturing** lambda (`lambda$createBabyArmorMeshSet$0(PartPose, CubeDeformation)`), so the `INVOKEDYNAMIC` chase accepts any handle producing a `MeshDefinition` and descends one hop into it.
  - **A baby draws `humanoid_baby` in all four slots** - leggings included, which is why vanilla ships no baby leggings sheet - **and never a trim.** `EquipmentLayerRenderer.renderLayers` returns before submitting one whenever the layer is `HUMANOID_BABY`, and `textures/trims/entity/` holds no baby atlas to sample.
  - The atlas is **read**, not assumed: `ArmorMeshIndex` takes it off `LayerDefinition.create(mesh, w, h)`, from the registration's `map` lambda for the sets that hand back bare meshes and from inside the factory for the armour stand's and the zombie villager's, which wrap their own.
- **The shell is textured through the cube's own unwrap, not a table.** `Unwrap.Atlas.crop` reads the rectangle out of its own `rect` - the same unwrap the entity body path resolves its UVs from - and `Turn.HALF_X` pairs each render-frame face with the model-frame one through the half turn the geometry already takes. On the **adult** shell this agrees with the skin table box for box, mirrored left limbs and all: `createMesh` gives `left_arm` / `left_leg` the right limb's `texOffs` with `mirror()`, which is exactly what the skin table's legacy left-limb fallback reconstructed. The crop is a nearest-neighbour resample rather than a rectangle copy, because the baby waist is `5.9 x 2 x 2.9` and its strip is not a whole number of texels; that is bit-identical to a copy on every other cube in the corpus. `HumanoidPart` still serves the **player** path, which dresses normalized body boxes rather than a mesh.
- **The crop must stay a cropped buffer: handing the rasterizer a sheet plus a rectangle instead is a SAMPLER change, not a container change.** `buildBox` gives each face a `W x H` buffer addressed by the full `[0,1]` UV rect, so a fetch is `(int)(u * W_crop)` and a read off the strip is transparent. Sampling the sheet directly makes it `(int)(u * W_sheet)` with `lastTexel` solved in sheet coordinates and out-of-sheet reads edge-clamped - different arithmetic on the player body, the player's own armour, the cape and all 14 armoured rows. **The 128 mirrored cubes are where the two provably part company**: the crop writes `buffer[j] = sheet[u0 + W - 1 - j]` while a mirrored UV interpolates a *descending* `u`, so the two land on different texels wherever an edge falls exactly on a sample - the same boundary class that made bounding the fetch to the face's own UV rect worth 31 block rows. `Unwrap.Atlas.crop` therefore survives on purpose, and `buildBox` calls it per face; folding it into the sampler is not the free deletion it looks like.
- **The canvas measures the shell, on both sides.** A shell stands clear of the body wherever vanilla inflates it, and a baby's is a hooded shroud around a body a third its bulk, so an unmeasured shell crops at the canvas edge. `ArmorKit.screenBounds` unions each equipped slot's alpha-tight bounds into the fit (gated on a piece actually being equipped, so an unarmoured render is untouched), and the harness's `EntityBoundsWalker` had to be taught to admit an `ArmorModelSet`'s components at all - the record is generic, so every component erases to `Object` and a declared-type test rejected all four. **The two computations share no code and agree to the pixel on all 14 armoured references.** A reference canvas that is the same size armoured as bare is the symptom of this bug, not evidence that vanilla clips.

### The face vocabulary splits on (corner phase, unwrap), NOT on block-versus-entity
There is **one** `Face` - six cardinal directions, one normal and one `Lighting.ITEMS_3D` shade scalar each - and the two things that vary per direction live on their own receivers, `CornerPhase` and `Unwrap`. They vary independently of each other and of the subject, and a caller picks one of each: `BlockGeometryKit.buildFromBones` - the **block-entity** path - takes `CornerPhase.POLYGON`, and `ShieldKit` - an **item** path - takes `CornerPhase.BAKERY` for its geometry and `Unwrap.Atlas` for its UV rectangle, and is correct. The subject being drawn picks neither.
- **`CornerPhase`** - which of a face's four corners the quad starts at, which fixes the diagonal the `(0,1,2)+(0,2,3)` fan splits on and which UV slot pairs with which vertex. Every one of the six `BAKERY` index arrays is a **cyclic rotation** of its `POLYGON` counterpart (never a reversal - solved exhaustively), so both name the same four points, hand them back in a different order, and split on **opposite** diagonals. Both are pinned to vanilla and neither is derivable from the other. **`BAKERY`'s UV pairing is the identity**, because its corner order *is* the `(TL, BL, BR, TR)` order `Vector4f.toUvCorners` returns - which is why it was never written down and why nothing pinned it. It is declared data now and both bakery call sites route through `permuteUv`, which is a no-op by construction rather than by arithmetic.
- **The fan is EMITTED in one place, and that place is `BlockGeometryKit.addQuad`.** `CornerPhase` decides which corner a quad starts at; the split into `(0,1,2)` + `(0,2,3)` is a separate act, and it was written **five** times - the block element path, the block bone path, `buildBox`, `FluidGeometryKit`, `ShieldKit` and `EntityGeometryKit`, agreeing by luck. Four of the five now call the one emitter, which takes the corner and uv quartets as the arrays their producers already hold. **What made the entity path joinable was making the shade an argument**: the emitter used to bake it from `(normal, directionalLight, lightEmission)`, which an entity cube cannot answer since its shade comes from its own light frame, so it now takes a `float shading` (16 parameters down to 9) and the element rule lives in `elementShade` - asked by the element path alone. A box, a bone cube, a fluid face and the shield bake `Lighting.inventory` at the call site, which is what the old `true, 0` literals hid. **The one site that cannot join is `FluidGeometryKit.addNonPlanarTop`**, and the reason is geometric rather than historical: a sloped fluid top's four corners are not coplanar, so its two halves face different ways and carry different normals and therefore different shades - there is no shared per-quad value to hand in. It splits on the same diagonal and says so in place. `QuadFanTest` pins all of it, and pins it *reachably* - flipping the emitter's second triangle onto the other diagonal fails 4 of its 5 tests, flipping the sloped top's fails exactly 1.
- **`Unwrap`** - `Element` reflects an element's *position* about 16 and never sees a size origin; `Atlas` never receives a position, so an inverted arm is structurally unreachable there. One signature would take both and ignore one. **`Atlas.rect` deliberately ignores its own `mirror` flag**: six entity-kit cull and translucency predicates ask what a face's *own* strip holds, so the mirrored strip is `Turn.MIRROR_X` applied by the caller, and only `crop` - which paints pixels rather than answering a predicate - applies both the swap and the texel flip itself.
- **What was derivable, and why deleting it was free.** `(widthAxis, heightAxis)` was 12 hand-written ints in *each* enum and is a function of `Face.axis()` (`Y -> (0,2)`, `Z -> (0,1)`, `X -> (2,1)`); the atlas V offset was two more columns, one all zeros and the other `1` exactly where `axis() != 1`. `0f * sx` is `+0.0f`, the surviving term cannot produce `-0.0f`, and `+0.0f + x == x`, so both deletions are bit-free rather than merely small. `axis()` itself is read off the normal's one non-zero component, so it cannot disagree with the direction it names.
- **There is deliberately no single normalising overload.** The divisor that turns a pixel rectangle into UVs means three different things - `16` for a block element *regardless of sprite resolution*, the shield's own texture size, and an entity model's **declared** texture size - so each arm returns pixels and the caller normalises where it knows the space. One method taking a scale that means three things would silently rescale every block-element UV by `16/spriteSize` on any HD resource pack.
- **The CTM grammar is a spelling, not a second direction vocabulary, and the refusal that said otherwise was void.** `CtmFace` was kept as a separate six-constant enum on the recorded grounds that "its tokens are parsed from resource-pack `.properties` files that must round-trip, so merging renames constants packs address by name". **The parse never used the constant names**: `parseSet` was an explicit lowercase-token switch (`case "top" -> TOP`) and never called `valueOf`, so renaming `TOP` would not have changed one character a pack writes. The stated mechanism was not in the code. `CtmContext.face` and `CtmRule.faces` are `Face` / `EnumSet<Face>` now and `PipelineRendererContext.resolveConnectedTexture` passes the render's face straight through. **The dialect lives in `CtmParser.parseFaceSet` and reaches no further** - all six tokens are written out there rather than deferring the four horizontals to `Face.fromName`, which they only match because OptiFine happens to spell them the way vanilla's model JSON does; keeping the table whole costs four lines and stops one method quietly serving two grammars. `CtmFace`'s declaration order was load-bearing nowhere (it was only ever an `EnumSet` member) where `Face`'s is load-bearing three times, so the merged order is `Face`'s at no cost. The six `fromFace` assertions became `CtmParserTest.faceTokenTable`, which pins the same relation from the side a pack can actually reach.

`FacePhaseTest` pins this: the index arrays verbatim, the CCW invariant on all twelve `(face, phase)` pairs, the fan diagonal as the corner-0/corner-2 pair, `BAKERY`'s identity pairing, and - the one that catches a half-applied rotation - that walking the real composition leaves `u` a function of the width-axis coordinate alone and `v` of the height-axis alone, with the per-face sign pair pinned. **`CornerPhase.vertexIndices` has no production reader** - `corners` covers those - so that test is its only one, which is the reason the accessor is public at all.

### The player skin unwrap and the entity cube unwrap are one function
`HumanoidPart`'s six skin rectangles per layer are **derived**, not tabulated: `Unwrap.Atlas.rect` at the part's atlas origin under `Turn.HALF_X`. All 72 coordinates and all 72 extents reproduce exactly. The overlay column is the same relation against a **different shipped cube** - the head's overlay origin `(32,0)` is vanilla's own `HumanoidModel#createBaseArmorMesh` `hat` cube and the torso's `(16,32)` its `AdultPiglinModel` `jacket`, verbatim - so the table was a second copy of shipped data rather than a third convention.
- **The `0.03` lattice rule: derive each endpoint from its own integer pixel offset, never `origin + extent * u`.** `0.03f` is `0.029999999329447746`; for `n` in `{2,4,8,16,32}` `n * 0.03f` is bit-identical to the shipped literal, but for `n = 12` it is **1 ULP low** against `0.36f` - and every limb is 12 px. The shipped constants dodged it by spelling endpoints and never a span; `HumanoidPart.box(u)` keeps that by storing `maxPx` rather than deriving it.
- **`centred(u)` is a separate method from `box(u)`, not the same box in another frame.** The skull scope draws one part alone and centres it (`+-0.5` for an 8 px head at `0.125` u/px) while `HEAD`'s lattice box is `y 8..16`, so `box(0.125f)` is **not** the unit cube.
- **The lattice rule's sibling: the SKULL arm's two box literals are derivable and its constant and its gate are not.** `HEAD` is 8 px on every axis, so `SKULL.boxOf(HEAD)` is `8f * 0.5f * 0.125f` = `0x3F000000` - **bit-identical to `BlockGeometryKit`'s unit cube** - and `0.5f + 0.02f` is `0x3F051EB8`, bit-identical to the literal `0.52f`. So the skull head and its hat overlay are one box and one `Box.expand`, verified in binary32 rather than argued. **What must NOT be shared is the constant or the predicate.** `0.02f` is `0.16` Minecraft pixels at the skull scope's `0.125` u/px where the body scopes' `OVERLAY_INFLATE = 0.01f` is `0.67` of one at `0.03` u/px - two calibrations, not one figure. And SKULL gates on `hasHatOverlay` (`w>=48, h>=16`) where `addBodyPart` gates on `hasOverlay` (`w>=64 && h>=64`), so unifying the gate deletes the hat layer on **every legacy 64x32 skin**. Sharing the arithmetic is free; sharing either of the other two is the change that stays deferred.
- **The 64x32 left-limb fallback is the production armour path, not a legacy corner** - the armour atlas *is* 64x32, so every armoured player render takes it on the left arm and the left leg. It cannot be folded into a turn or a mirror flag: it substitutes a **different part's** atlas origin, which no permutation of the part's own strip can reach. `HumanoidPartCropTest` is the only gate that sees it.
- **A scope's pixel extent is the union of its parts' boxes**, so `PlayerOptions.Type`'s declared `bodyHeight`/`bodyWidth` were a sixth encoding of the same table: SKULL `8x8`, BUST `20x16`, FULL `32x16` all fall out exactly. The 2D layout falls out too - canvas X is a part's left edge minus the scope's, canvas Y the scope's top minus the part's top (the one inversion, since the lattice counts Y up and a canvas counts it down). **Both layouts are the scope's own answers now** - `Type.boxes()` seats each part in the scope's model frame and is tabulated once per constant rather than assembled per render, and `Type.layout2D(canvasSize)` places each part's canvas rectangle. That is what let the six public pixel / extent accessors go: their only readers were the two free functions that moved onto the enum.

### Frame turns are one closed group, and it must admit reflections
Every frame relation the renderer performs pairs each face either with itself or with its own opposite, so **every one is `diag(+-1,+-1,+-1)`** - a member of the order-8 diagonal group - and a ninety-degree turn appears nowhere. `face.Turn` is that group: eight constants named by which axes they negate, with `apply` on a point and on a face, `then` for composition, and `reflects`.
- **Only four distinct elements are in use**: the identity, `HALF_X` = `diag(1,-1,-1)` (the model-frame to upright-frame relation), `MIRROR_Y` = `diag(1,-1,1)` (the shading flip), and `MIRROR_X` = `diag(-1,1,1)` (the cube `mirror` flag's face swap).
- **An `SO(3)`-only abstraction cannot express this.** Four of the relations are reflections - the two cube mirrors, the shading flip and the cape's face assignment - so restricting the set to rotations would express none of them. Closure matters too: a mirrored shell cube is `HALF_X.then(MIRROR_X)`, not a ninth relation.
- **The face map reads a face's axis off its normal and its opposite off one bit of its ordinal**, since `Face` declares its constants in opposing pairs (`DOWN, UP` on Y; `NORTH, SOUTH` on Z; `WEST, EAST` on X). That layout is asserted rather than assumed.
- **Naming the relation does NOT make divergence unrepresentable, and the claim should not be made.** `Turn.HALF_X.apply(...)` and a `Turn.HALF_X` stored elsewhere are still two literal mentions and editing one compiles. What it buys is that the drift is one greppable token instead of a `Map.of` in one package and a `switch` in another, with `FrameTurnTest` pinning the value.
- **Two different turns can hide in one function.** `ArmorKit.intoModelFrame` applies `HALF_X` to the geometry and then a further `MIRROR_Y` to the shading normal, because the kit's tuned light frame is reflected in Y from the geometry frame. The old name (`turnAboutX`) promised one turn while performing two.

### Iso pose (VANILLA_ISO + renderer-owned facing)
- All iso subjects (block, fluid, portal, player, entity) share `Projection.VANILLA_ISO` = `(30°, 225°, 0°)` + `Lens.ISOMETRIC_BLOCK` (vanilla's `display.gui` pose/scale, technically a dimetric). It is **facing-neutral** - presents the model's `-Z` side. (`Projection` is the sole owner of these poses; `EulerRotation.STANDARD_*` is gone.)
- **Facing is per-renderer**, applied as a model-to-world `Placement` (see `engine.camera.Placement`, composed by `ModelEngine` as `pose · placement · modelSpin`): block/fluid/portal = `IDENTITY`; player = `R_Y(180)`; entity = `R_Y(180)·flip180 = R_Z(180) = diag(-1,-1,1)` (which also un-flips its Y-down model). The camera is a plain **det=+1** display pose; the entity's canvas-fit measures its silhouette through `ModelEngine.orient(spin)` (= `pose · ENTITY_FACING · spin`, the exact render orientation) and hands it to `ModelEngine.rasterizeFitted` via a `FitRequest` (see below). This is what lets any projection be swapped in and still present the subject's front, upright.
- The entity's harness angle `(210°, 45°, 0°)` (`EntityFrameRenderer.ISO_ROTATION`) survives only as `EntityGeometryKit.ENTITY_ISO_LIGHTING` - the plane-cube lighting frame - decoupled from the camera pose. The old fused `det=-1` `entityIsoChain` / `ENTITY_ISO` assembly are **deleted**; the kit is de-flipped (emits Y-up geometry, det=+1 internally).
- Byte-identity is pinned by `VanillaEntityTransformGoldenTest` (the `VANILLA_ISO` pose 16 floats + kit-fixture corners) and `EntityGeometryKitTest` - whose winding invariant is now **"emit-order cross AGREES with the stored normal"** (the kit is det=+1 internally; the chirality reflection re-enters via the `Placement`, so screen-space cull winding is unchanged). Run both before/after any kit or camera change; the entity parity sweep must hold too.

### Canvas fit (unified: player + entity, one authority)
`ModelEngine.rasterizeFitted` + `engine.camera.FitRequest` are the single fit path for player and entity (block/fluid/portal render a unit cube at fixed scale, no fit). The kit emits **fit-neutral** geometry; scale + centring live only in the engine's `prepareFit`, forking on the request mode and the lens kind:
- `FitRequest.autoFill(fill)` - engine measures the triangle silhouette and fills `fill` of the canvas. **Orthographic** bakes a 3D `scale(fit)` (keeps the depth frame - the window-depth grid below is a function of the canvas scale, so it is not scale-invariant); **perspective/oblique** carry a 2D post-projection `Fit2D`. Used by the player and by the entity's perspective/oblique path (fed **unit-normalized** geometry via `EntityGeometryKit.unitFit` so foreshortening stays well-behaved). This is what fits long entities (cod) uncropped under PORTRAIT / cavalier / cabinet / military - a 3D model-scale fit could not correct strong foreshortening in one pass.
- `FitRequest.nativeScale(ndcScale, projectedBounds)` - caller supplies its native pixels-per-block scale + a pre-measured **alpha-tight (optionally family-unioned)** silhouette box (measured through `ModelEngine.orient`); engine bakes the scale in 3D and centres the box midpoint in screen space. This is the entity's **orthographic VANILLA_ISO** path; parity is byte-stable (re-baseline was a no-op, max drift +0.004, snap-absorbed). Byte-identity of the player auto-fill arms is pinned by `PlayerRasterizeFittedGoldenTest`.

### Depth, the coverage snap, and coplanar ties (load-bearing)
**The `1/400` coverage snap (`ModelEngine.snapToCoverageGrid`) is a coverage device and must never move depth.** It perturbs each projected vertex sub-pixel to dodge exact-alignment GPU-vs-software divergence, and the rasterizer's coverage and UV interpolation are tuned around it. Depth is not: `depthOnUnsnappedPlane` re-reads each vertex's depth off its triangle's own **unsnapped** plane at its **snapped** screen position, so barycentric interpolation over the snapped triangle reproduces the unsnapped plane exactly. Skipping that step pairs snapped barycentrics with unsnapped `z`, which tilts every triangle's depth plane by an amount derived from its own vertices - and since depth here is affine in screen space with no perspective-correct path, two *genuinely coplanar* triangles then disagree by a systematic bias (measured at **+56 to +70 ULP** on the worn-armour chestplate seam, enough to win `GL_LEQUAL` outright in either draw order). The solve is in `double` on purpose: its whole job is to leave no systematic residue between two triangles of one plane.
- **`Projected.z0/z1/z2` is the raster depth; `p0/p1/p2.z()` stays the true camera-space depth** the translucent `quadDepthKey` sort reads. Do not collapse them.
- **Depth is compared on vanilla's grid, not on ours, and that grid is measured rather than assumed.** A harness probe (`renderVanillaReferences -PrefharnessDepthQuantumProbe=true`, output outside the reference tree) renders two overlapping quads a swept `2^-k` of the window range apart and reads which one the depth test kept: the transition sits at **`2^-23`** at the middle of the range, identically at depth range `1000` and `10000`, and one step finer off-centre. So the quantum is a `float32` ULP beside `0.5` and is range-independent in *window* units - the assumed model. The unorm32-storage alternative predicted `2^-32` (out by 256x, and it would have inverted the analysis) and the clip-z-only-float32 alternative predicted no transition at all at the centre. `ModelEngine.onVanillaDepthGrid` rounds each interpolated fragment depth through `0.5f - depth*k` and back, `k = scale / (2 * VANILLA_DEPTH_RANGE)` with `VANILLA_DEPTH_RANGE = 1000` - vanilla's own `PictureInPictureRenderer` ortho half-extent. Vanilla's viewport transform lands every window depth beside `0.5`, where a `float` step is `2^-24`, so forming that value costs about six bits and sets the floor below which vanilla cannot tell two surfaces apart. Raw camera-space `z` resolves **58x to 224x finer** (`32000/minDim`), which decides coplanar contests outright that vanilla leaves to draw order. **Round the interpolated depth, never the three vertex depths** - rounding at the vertices tilts each triangle's depth plane by its own vertices' rounding, which is exactly the error `depthOnUnsnappedPlane` exists to remove, and it costs `+3.84` fleet. Overridable for sweeps via `-Dasset.depth.range=N`; the optimum over `125..4000` sits on `1000`, so it is derived rather than fitted.
- **Depth comes off a per-triangle `float` plane, not per-pixel barycentrics.** Two `float` gradients solved once per triangle from the snapped positions, evaluated as `z0 + dzdx*dx + dzdy*dy` - the same plane, rounded where hardware rounds it. Solving once gives two coplanar triangles slightly different gradients, so their depth planes cross inside an overlap. A perspective lens keeps the barycentric form.
- **That crossing is mostly quantised away, and it was measured rather than assumed.** Over 66 coplanar seams harvested from 31 subjects, **74%** of the decided seam pixels (42,655 of 57,394) land on *exactly* equal depth once rounded onto vanilla's grid, so draw order alone settles them; only the remaining 26% are decided by the planes. The two mechanisms pull against each other by construction - the grid quantum is coarser than the residue two `float` plane solutions of one plane differ by - and the consequence is that **the winner is a single constant across 65 of the 66 seams**. Where the planes do decide, they are right **87%** of the time against **67%** on the ties, so the crossing helps where it fires; it simply does not fire often. Frozen in `notes/refharness-unify/probes/L29-tie-verdicts.tsv`.
- **No draw order can match vanilla's seam verdicts, and the counter-example is inside one subject.** All 14 wearers dress in the same shell, drawn in the same order, yet vanilla shows the *arm* over the chestplate on the right and the *torso* on the left for the bogged, the giant and the husk. A per-seam oracle - the best constant answer for each seam, which no single order can deliver - would reach 95.4% against the 66.6% we get; the gap to 100% is vanilla's own GPU dither. Draw order is a ceiling, not a lever.
- **There is no depth tolerance anywhere.** `depthFails` is a bare `depthVal < existingDepth`. The emissive slack is deleted: swept across four orders of magnitude it decided 2 rows of 401 and both were closer without it. **A coincident overlay is not separated either** - the `0.001` clearance inflate is deleted; a same-mesh grow-less overlay draws coincident with its base exactly as vanilla submits it, and wins on `GL_LEQUAL` by drawing second. Deleting it moved 59 rows, every one closer, zero regressions. **An armour trim is not separated either** - `ArmorKit` builds it on the shell's own box and lets the later draw carry it, which is what vanilla does: the trim is submitted on the same model and pose one order slot later, and every one of the 18 registered patterns takes the ordinary armour cutout rather than the equal-depth-no-write decal pipeline, because the single helper that registers them passes `decal = false`. Deleting the two inflates changed no pixel's alpha on the trim sheet's 11 cells and moved 0 of 401 entity rows.
- **A coplanar pair is decided by emission order, last-drawn-wins** (`depthFails` passes an equal depth - vanilla `GL_LEQUAL`). `EntityModelData.getBones()` order therefore *is* the tied-depth priority, which is why Pass 1 preserves encounter order and non-translucent triangles are never sorted. It is a `ConcurrentLinkedMap`, insertion-ordered by construction - do not swap it for a hash map. That order is the tooling's `addOrReplaceChild` declaration order, **not** vanilla's `PartDefinition.children` `HashMap` bake order, and reproducing the bake order was built, measured and **declined three times**: a near-neutral re-roll before the depth comparison was fixed, `+7.53` fleet with the armour band `0.3791 -> 0.9168` after, and then each half on its own. The halves are exactly additive and they are not alike - the three shared armour meshes cost `+7.5286` (13 rows worse against 1 better), the other 138 cost `+0.0035` (9 better, 10 worse, cancelling). **The entity half's `-0.660` gain is gone**: under the old comparison it was the best number on the board and the case for carving it out; under a faithful one it is a coin toss that pays nothing, which is what the carve-out was refused for in the first place. **Do not re-open this by reordering bones** - frozen in `notes/refharness-unify/baselines/probe-L27-bake-order*.tsv`, `probe-L28-L27-bake-order-rerun.tsv` and `probe-L29-bake-order-{entity,armour}-only.tsv`.
- **First-drawn-wins is not a live alternative.** Re-swept once ties were real: `<=` gives `2262.96` against `143.9972`. Vanilla's `GL_LEQUAL` is right by a factor of sixteen.
- **On an ADDITIVE pass a coplanar tie is not a tie-break, it is a doubling - and it is still unreachable.** `grow` puts adjacent inflated boxes through each other, so the charged aura's like-facing quads are genuinely coplanar; there are exactly **7** such pairs on `creeper~charged=true` and they carry **79.3%** of that row. `GL_LEQUAL` passes an equal depth, so both accumulate, and vanilla does the same - it draws both on **83%** of the 7,833 contests (over 7,818 pixels). What decides the other 17% is float error vanilla has and this renderer does not, and three separate measurements say it cannot be recovered. **The disagreements point both ways** (java draws both where vanilla draws one on the front legs' `north` faces, one where vanilla draws both on their `up` faces), so no constant rule fixes the row. **Five of the seven pairs are structurally identical** - mirror bones, same face, same inflate - and vanilla resolves four "both" and the fifth "one". And **java's own residue anti-predicts**: past `0.25` of a depth quantum, where java's two planes disagree most, vanilla says "same surface" **100%** of the time. Best threshold on it: 83.0%, which *is* that constant; per-pair oracle 88.4%; landed 71.8%. Do not re-open this with a comparison rule - frozen in `notes/refharness-unify/probes/L37-creeper-aura-contests.tsv`.
  - **Rounding onto the grid manufactures a full-quantum gap out of a sub-quantum one** whenever the pair straddles a grid line, at a rate equal to the residue in quanta - java's both-rate tracks `1 - residue/Q` across all seven pairs. Replacing the rounding with a one-quantum **tolerance** was built and **declined**: a depth buffer stores a quantised value and compares it exactly, so slack is a fitted constant of the kind L28 deleted two of, and it buys the creeper `0.62` by moving 18 rows the wrong way and pushing `drowned~age=baby` over 1.0. Per-pixel **barycentric** depth instead of the per-triangle plane is falsified outright (row `-0.03`, fleet `+0.57`), so the residue is not an artefact of solving the plane once per triangle.
- **The canvas-centre column was its own defect, and it fired only on an ODD canvas width - which is why no canvas is odd any more (see the close two bullets down).** The fit centres the subject, so a symmetric model's front vertical corner lands on the canvas axis - at an odd width that axis is exactly a pixel centre, and the screen edge where its two faces meet passes exactly through a sample point. **36 of 262 odd-width rows put `>=25%` of their whole error on that one column** (up to `85.4%`, and it is the single heaviest column in 63 of them) against **0 of 136** even-width rows. Corpus-wide it is `3.8%` of the entity sweep. **It is not the coverage snap**: across `-Dasset.snap.grid=400 / 0 / 4096` the plain creeper's mass off that column is `13,646` at all three, byte-identical, and turning the snap off makes the column slightly worse. **Nor is it the fit, and moving it is a regression rather than a fix** - per-column coverage difference against vanilla is exactly `0` on `creeper`, `pillager` and `slime`, so vanilla's axis is on the *same* sample point ours is; the canvas size is the harness's and the reference is centred the same way, so a top-left origin would move all 402 rows off vanilla's placement to dissolve one collision. It is not a bounds-accuracy problem either - the bounds are alpha-tight and agree to the column, and the axis landing on a sample is a consequence of getting them *right* on a symmetric subject (`slime`: `0` asymmetric silhouette px, `0` column difference, `67.5%` of its error on that column). The alignment is shared; what differs is who owns a pixel whose edge function is exactly `-0.0`, and which texel is read when `u` lands exactly on a texel boundary. Per-row shares and width parity are frozen in `notes/refharness-unify/probes/L37-centre-column-share.tsv`, re-measured after the two fixes below as `L38-centre-column-share.tsv`.
- **The alignment at that column is exact by construction on BOTH sides, so it is settled by rules rather than by float.** A symmetric model's front corner has model `x` of exactly `0`, so it projects to `0 * scale + offsetX` - `offsetX` exactly, and the dump reads `s0=90.5,0.42` on a 181-wide canvas. **The even-width rows are the control that proves vanilla is exact too**: float noise of any size would put the corner in one pixel or the other where it lands on a *boundary*, disagreeing by a whole column, and `0 of 136` even rows do. **The `1/400` snap therefore cannot reach this case at all** - `90.5` is already on that grid, so snapping returns it unchanged. Two rules own the sample:
  - **The fill rule must be classified on the SIGN-NORMALIZED edge direction.** `EdgeCoefficients.of` negates the coefficients when `denom < 0`, and negating an edge's coefficients *is* reversing its direction (`e_ji == -e_ij` identically), so the top-left flags are negated with them. The predicate's arms follow from the winding rather than being free: with `e >= 0` marking the interior (clockwise, Y down), `e_ij >= 0` puts the interior to the **left** of a downward edge, so a **left edge goes UP** (`ey < sy`) and a **top edge goes RIGHT** (`ex > sx`). The mirrored reading is equally self-consistent - still exactly one owner per shared edge - but it is the bottom-right rule and hands the sample to the opposite face from the GPU.
  - **A fetch may not step outside the face's own UV rectangle.** `(int)(u * w)` maps `[t, t+1)` to `t`, which is right strictly inside a face and steps one texel past at its closed upper edge - a value the interpolation produces exactly when that edge lands on a sample. That texel belongs to the neighbouring face in the sheet. `ModelEngine.lastTexel` bounds it via `ceil(uMax * w) - 1`.
- **At the face's LOWER UV bound the same correction is NOT sign-robust, and that half is unreachable.** At `uMax`, vanilla lands on the same texel whether it sits exactly on the boundary or a hair below - which is why bounding the fetch holds at **96.6 - 100%** on and off the centre column and moved 31 block rows with zero regressions. At `uMin` the two readings give different texels, and the corpus flips on position: **94.4%** "take the lower texel" on the centre column against **0.2%** everywhere else, over the same uv class, axis, face kind and coverage-tie flag (`slime`'s `cube:north` at `ux=8.0` and `squid`'s `body:north` at `ux=12.0` resolve opposite ways). Nothing the fetch holds separates them, and it is not a snap artefact - at `-Dasset.snap.grid=0` the squid's `tentacle3:west` still interpolates `u = 0.75` exactly. **Taking that band anyway (`ceil(x)-1` unconditionally) is declined**: it is worth `0.68` on the entity sum but reads into transparent sheet padding on the squid and deletes a whole column of silhouette vanilla draws (`java-cov` `0.7312 -> 0.7305`). Frozen in `probes/L38-texel-tie-verdicts.tsv`.
- **Unreachable *from the fetch* is not the same as nothing to do - the tie is manufactured, and both sides manufacture it identically.** Java's `prepareFit` NATIVE_SCALE arm centres the measured bounds and the projection adds `offsetX = width * 0.5f`; the harness's `EntityFrameRenderer` does `translateX = scope.width() / 2.0f - anchorX * scale`. A symmetric subject's front corner **is** the anchor, so it lands on `w/2` **regardless of its extent** - a pixel centre at odd `w`, a pixel boundary at even `w`. One model at three canvas sizes shows it with nothing else varying: `slime` `181`/odd **62.8%** centre share against `362`/even `0.7%` and `724`/even `0.4%`; `magma_cube` `182`/even **0.0%** against `363`/odd `48.4%` and `725`/odd `31.6%`. **Both sides now round the canvas width up to even, and the column is gone rather than reduced** (`EntityRenderer.evenWidth` + the bump in the harness's `EntitySweep`): rows with `>=25%` of their error on that column went **36 to 0**, the centre is the peak column in **0** rows, and the corpus's centre-column mass went `3.27% -> 0.57%` - the even population's own share. Entity sum `64.0493 -> 60.0043`, buckets `302/393/397/401 -> 306/394/400/401`, `creeper~charged=true` `2.0951 -> 1.3888` and again the only row at or above 1.0; block / item / armour / player reproduce to the digit. It beat its own `-1.69` estimate and its `-2.03` ceiling, because the off-centre mass improved on net rather than merely holding. **The placement was the harness's convention, not vanilla's, which is what made this legitimate**: `EntitySweep` sizes the canvas from its own alpha-tight bounds at its own 256 px/block and centres in a PiP scope it created, and vanilla never renders an entity into a `181x202` frame. Only the **width** - a subject is symmetric left to right, not top to bottom. It moves **265 of 2311** references (every one under `entities/`, exactly the odd-width population), so it needs both repos in lockstep and a full `renderVanillaAllReferences`; `refs-L39.sha256` is the manifest.
- **A half-pixel re-align re-samples every pixel, and what that exposed was a harness bug worth its own fix.** Six large-shape `tropical_fish` rows regressed on a **detached 1-px vertical line vanilla drew and java did not** - at one row it was the only opaque pixel in the whole row, 55 px from the tail. It is not geometry: the harness's own `[PX]` dump (`-PentityPixelDump`) puts vanilla's fin edge at `x=23.480406` against java's `23.48`, agreeing to `0.001` px. **An overlay layer that inflates a plane cube turns its four collapsing edge faces into real slivers that still carry a zero-width UV strip** - `CubeDeformation` moves the corner positions but not the unwrap, which comes off the authored size - so the GPU rasterizes a surface with no texture extent, sampling the degenerate strip at `u = texOffs.u + sz`. The fish's `grow 0.008` is `0.181` px at 256 px/block: invisible in game at any real entity size, wide enough here to catch a sample. `EntityBoundsWalker` **already** dropped those polygons from the canvas measurement ("they render no pixels" - true unless inflated), so the harness disagreed with itself; `DegenerateUvPolygonMixin` filters them at `ModelPart$Cube` construction so render and measurement read the same array. 8 references moved, all better, entity `62.2581 -> 60.0043`. **Emitting the slivers on the asset side instead was built and declined**: `isDegeneratePlaneFace` judges flatness on `cube.getSize()` while the corners come from the inflated `scaledCubeBounds`, and judging on the grown extent does emit them - but it recovered none of the six rows and cost `+0.0118` on two others, because java's barycentric coverage of a fifth-of-a-pixel sliver does not agree with a GPU's edge functions (the fin's `down` sliver solves to bary `-83`). The skip is a deliberate compensation for that, not an oversight.
- **Whether a pass writes depth and whether its quads are sorted are two declarations vanilla makes, and neither follows from `emissive`.** `emissive` is the `EMISSIVE` shader define; the write is the pipeline's `DepthStencilState.writeDepth`; the sort is the render type's `RenderSetup.sortOnUpload`. Vanilla splits them every way, and the breeze splits them **inside one entity** - its eyes pass does not write and its wind pass does - so any rule reading one off another fails on a single subject. All three ride the overlay's `pipeline` node, walked by `EntityPipelineTraits`, and they travel from the decode to the fragment as one `PassDeclaration(emissive, blend, alpha, writesDepth, sorted)` - the value `Entity.OverlayLayer`, `EntityBuildParams` and `SurfaceTraits` each carry one of rather than five loose members apiece. `depth_write` omits its `true` identity (what `DepthStencilState.DEFAULT` declares) and `sorted` its `false`. A block declaring no depth state **inherits the snippet's**, which is the only way `BREEZE_WIND` resolves at all. Pinned by `OverlayPipelineRosterTest`.
  - **The split between the pass and the surface is per-pass against per-cube, and it is what makes one record possible.** `SurfaceTraits`' other three members - `cullBackFaces`, `translucent`, `glinted` - are decided **per cube inside the build** from that cube's own geometry and texels, so they cannot ride a record a caller hands in, and the pass's five cannot be decided per cube. Handing `EntityBuildParams` a whole `SurfaceTraits` instead was built and abandoned for exactly that reason: three of its components have no answer at that boundary. The corollary is that `SurfaceTraits` has **one** constructor, not a convenience beside it - a second four-argument form would put `boolean` in the same first three positions with `emissive` and `translucent` swapped, so a transposition would compile silently.
  - **`PipelineParityDump` names `emissive` / `blend` / `alpha` as string literals beside the reads**, so re-homing those members changes the Java and not one emitted byte - unlike the `ADDITIVE` -> `ADD` rename, which moved a dump label. The dump is nonetheless a live gate for the values: forcing the `alpha` decode to `1f` moves the warden overlay `0.25 -> 1.0` in both `entities.json` copies and both `manifest.sha256`. Diff the dump, not just the sweeps, for any change to how a pass is decoded.
  - **A self-occluding pass needs both halves, and each alone is worthless.** Keying the write on `emissive` meant no aura fragment could occlude another, so the charged shell - the body mesh at `grow 2.0`, drawn two-sided - added its far faces on top of its near ones. Depth write alone measures `2.6069 -> 16.13` on `creeper~charged=true` because the fragments arrive farthest-first and `GL_LEQUAL` passes all of them; the sort alone is exactly neutral, since saturating addition does not care about order. Together they reproduce the reference **to the byte** at the diagnosed pixel, and the pair row `wither~charged=true` goes `0.5217 -> 0.0128`.
  - **The sort is by quad centroid, and that is an approximation of a per-pixel order rather than a weaker form of one.** A quad drawn later can still lose the test over the part of its area that lies behind an earlier one - which is exactly how vanilla drops the aura head's top face where it passes behind the body, while keeping it everywhere else.
  - **`quadDepthKey` identifies the shared diagonal in camera space, never on screen.** An iso projection turns a square face into a rhombus whose short diagonal is no longer than its sides, so a screen-space longest-edge test picks a different edge for each half of such a quad and splits it; the halves then sort apart and, once the pass also writes depth, fight along the diagonal they share (`4.55` against `2.2019`). In camera space a cube face is still a rectangle. On its own the fix is a no-op - `65.2083 -> 65.2070`, one row.
- **`RendererDebug.pixelWrite` logs a COLOUR write and the CANDIDATE depth - never the stored one.** A `WRITE` line therefore proves only that the fragment passed and composited, and a reader who takes it for a depth write will conclude the buffer held a value it never held. Nothing in the standard dump prints `depth[idx]` at the moment of the test; add a temporary probe when that is the question. Two pixels from different renders are also easy to conflate - the same subject's charged and uncharged rows have different canvases (`317x563` against `227x463`) and different depth arrays, so check the width a dumped `idx` implies before comparing two lines.
- **The armour shell's triangles carry a `debugTag` too**, passed into `BlockGeometryKit.buildBox` as `part:face` (the `HumanoidPart` name on the skin path, suffixed `_overlay` on the helmet's second box; the bone name on the mesh path) only when `RendererDebug.tracingPixels()` reports `-Dasset.entity.pixel.dump` armed. It is an **argument**, not a value recovered downstream from emission order - the old form re-derived each triangle's face as `CACHED_VALUES[index / 2]`, which is a correct reading of the emitter and a dependency nothing pinned. Without it armour fragments log `tag=null` and `pixelTriangle` skips them entirely, which has misread the armour seam twice.
- Blocks/items are structurally immune (unit cube at fixed scale, no coplanar sibling boxes): a depth change that moves entities leaves **0 of 1055** block and **0 of 479** item rows. That held for the depth-comparison change too, and it is the mechanism working rather than missing them - their coplanar pairs are exactly coincident, so both interpolation forms agree bit-for-bit and there is no crossing to find. Use it as the discriminator when a rasterizer change moves something unexpected. **The immunity is to DEPTH and does not generalise**: a coverage or texel-fetch change reaches blocks like anything else - bounding the fetch to the face's own UV rect moved 31 block rows (all better, `dragon_head` `0.3919 -> 0.1702`), so treat the block and item sums as a live gate for those, not a formality.
- **The reference set's own depth range is part of the contract.** `EntityFrameRenderer.DEPTH_RANGE` is `1000`, matching every other harness frame renderer and vanilla's PiP. It was `10000`, which recorded the entity ground truth at ten times coarser depth than vanilla's own GUI path. **A change that emulates the reference's rounding cannot be evaluated against a reference whose rounding is coarser than the one being emulated** - the same two changes measured as regressions at `10000` and as wins at `1000`. Fix the ground truth first. `PlayerFrameRenderer.DEPTH_RANGE` is `1000` too now - it was the last `10000` in the harness, and closing it moved `skull` not at all and `full` by **22 px of 262,144**, a two-pixel band of one texel's shade levels with no alpha change. That is a coplanar seam between the player's own body boxes: the player is *nearly* immune, not structurally immune, so diff the references before re-pinning rather than after.
- **The sweep is exactly reproducible** - four fresh JVM forks, 0 of 401 rows different, sum spread `0.0000`. A moved row is a real change, never run noise.

### JOML factory conventions (load-bearing)
JOML's `Quaternionf` has two Tait-Bryan factories with OPPOSITE application order. Vanilla uses both:

| Factory | Quaternion product | Visual application to `v` | Vanilla site |
|---|---|---|---|
| `rotationZYX(z, y, x)` | `q_z·q_y·q_x` | **X first**, then Y, then Z | `ModelPart.translateAndRotate` (bone rotations) |
| `rotationXYZ(x, y, z)` | `q_x·q_y·q_z` | **Z first**, then Y, then X | GUI `display.*` poses (block icon, harness `ISO_ROTATION`) |

The factory NAME orders the quaternion product; application order to `v` is REVERSED because `q · v · q^-1` composes right-to-left. Mixing these up was the Round 5 bone-rotation bug.

Row-form equivalents (this codebase's `v_row × M`; `Matrix4f.createRotationX(θ)` produces a visual `+θ` X-rotation):
- **Bone rotations** (`rotationZYX`, X-first): `createRotationX(pitch).multiply(createRotationY(yaw)).multiply(createRotationZ(roll))` - locked in `EntityGeometryKitJava.pivotCenteredRotation`.
- **GUI display poses** (`rotationXYZ`, Z-first): `createRotationZ(roll).multiply(createRotationY(yaw)).multiply(createRotationX(pitch))` - used by `Camera.fromPose` (the `display.*` pose builder, assembled into named poses by `Projection`'s `VANILLA_*` members).

A harness change to that renderer therefore leaves them holding ground truth recorded by the old code, and that happened twice - 120 armour-glint frames once, and the four `armor/minecraft__zombie_*` rows left behind when the entity references were re-rendered at vanilla's depth range. `renderVanillaAllReferences` drives every sweep in **one** client boot, which is what closes the gap: measured at **152s** against 125s for the incomplete sweep, and 43s *cheaper* than the three narrower tasks run separately (125 + 43 + 27), since it pays the client boot once. The narrow tasks stay for scoped iteration.
- Hash the whole tree before and after, not just the sub-tree the task you ran writes. `notes/refharness-unify/baselines/refs-L39.sha256` is the current 2311-entry manifest.
- **A reference that moves on a re-render with your change stashed was stale, not moved** - that is the check that separates the two, and two consecutive runs agreeing byte for byte is what rules out flapping.
- **A depth-range change reaches a subject only if it has a coplanar seam inside the band the change spans**, so "this sweep did not move" is never evidence a sweep was unaffected. The armour sweep's zombies moved at `10000 -> 1000` and its piglins did not; the piglins move too at `100000`. Both families have contested seams and the piglins' are merely wider, so the split is a matter of degree rather than of kind.

### Per-renderer override gotchas
When porting a new entity, ALWAYS check if its renderer overrides `setupRotations` or has a scale override - replicate in the asset-renderer kit's transform chain.

- **`setupRotations` overrides (14 in 26.1)**: ArmorStand, Cat, Cod, Drowned, Fox, IronGolem, Panda, Phantom, Pufferfish, Salmon, Shulker, Squid, TropicalFish, **AvatarRenderer** (the player - no parity rows).
- **`setupRotations` TRANSLATES - only two survive the harness rest pose.** Everything else is behind a gate that is false there (cat `lieDownAmount`, panda `rollTime`/`sitAmount`, cod/salmon/tropical_fish `!isInWater` which `FreezeAnimationStateMixin` forces true, iron_golem's early return, avatar `isVisuallySwimming`). The two live ones:
  - **Squid** - age-conditional, `translate(0, isBaby ? 0.25 : 0.5, 0)` before the rotations and `translate(0, isBaby ? -0.6 : -1.2, 0)` after, netting **-0.7 blocks adult / -0.35 baby**. Emitted as `axes.age.options.<age>.y_shift` and applied to the MESH at load (`EntityIndexBuilder.shiftModel`), not as a render parameter - the renderer and the canvas-sizing bounds walk both read the mesh, so moving it is what stops them disagreeing. The two translates sum only because `xBodyRot`/`zBodyRot` are uninitialised on a never-ticked entity, so every rotation between them is about Y; the tooling declines a literal non-Y turn for that reason.
  - **Pufferfish** - `translate(0, Mth.cos(ageInTicks*0.05)*0.08, 0)`, an expression rather than a literal, so the walk declines it. **Latent, not armed**: its three sizes have three different canvases, so each per-subject fit absorbs it.
- **A `setupRotations` shift is invisible unless the canvas is group-unioned.** `ModelEngine.prepareFit`'s NATIVE_SCALE arm centres the bounds it was handed, so a translate applied to both geometry and bounds cancels exactly for a subject measured alone. squid/glow_squid is the only grouped entity in the corpus whose renderer translates - which is why its adults did not move when the shift landed.
- **`scale(state, ps)` overrides**: WitherBoss → 2×, Zoglin (when `isBaby`) → ~0.5×. **The giant's 6× is not one of these** - it is a `MeshTransformer` (below), and its geometry key says so: `HumanoidModel#createMesh@scaled=6.0`.
- **`MeshTransformer.scaling(F)` (LayerDefinition-time scale)** is **baked by the tooling and exact** - do not go looking for a runtime workaround. `RENDERER_SCALE_OVERRIDES` no longer exists, and no model in `entity_models.json` carries a `renderer_scale`; the factor rides the geometry key as `@scaled=F` and the mesh is emitted already scaled. Bytecode semantics, and they are narrower than they look: `mesh.transformed(pose -> pose.scaled(F).translated(0, 24.016*(1-F), 0))` rewrites **only the `MeshDefinition` root**, because `PartDefinition.transformed` copies its children by reference untransformed. So the whole transformer collapses to root pivot `(0, 24.016*(1-F), 0)` and root scale `F`. `GeometryParser.applyMeshTransformerScaling` reproduces it to `0.000000` model units on all three whole-mesh users, and `BoneKit`'s non-propagating bone scale is its exact algebraic inverse at every chain depth. Whole-mesh catalog (26.1): giant 6.0, husk 1.0625, wither_skeleton 1.2, plus `PolarBearModel` 1.2, `HappyGhastModel` 4.0, `GhastModel` 4.5, `GuardianModel` 2.35 (`ELDER_GUARDIAN_SCALE`).
- **`PartPose.scaled(F)` scales the pivot as well as the three scale components** - six fields, not three - and `PartPose.withScale(F)` scales only the three. Both are parsed, and the asymmetry is deliberate because it is vanilla's. The per-bone path missed `scaled`'s pivot half until it was fixed; the corpus's only user is the dragon head, whose pose is `offset(0, -7.986666, 0).scaled(0.75)`, and `withScale`'s four vanilla users (`EndCrystalModel` ×2, `ArrowModel`, `SpinAttackEffectModel`) are in neither geometry file.
- **`BabyModelTransform` is the second whole-mesh transformer, and it is not a scale.** Seven values against `scaling(F)`'s one, and it rewrites a pose **per top-level bone** through one of two operators chosen by name: the parts it names take `translated(0, yHead, zHead).scaled(1.5/headScale)` and everything else `translated(0, bodyY, 0).scaled(1/bodyScale)`. Same "only the root's direct children move" mechanism as `scaling(F)` - `PartDefinition.transformed` copies children by reference - so a grandchild keeps its own pose and rides its top-level ancestor's, which is why `GeometryParser.applyBabyMeshTransform` resolves each bone's factor from its **top-level ancestor**. The translate happens **before** the scale (`(pivot + offset) * f`), which is the opposite order from `scaling(F)`'s `scaled().translated()`. It rides `GeometryRequest.babyTransform` rather than folding into the scale, and the key names the constant - `@baby=HumanoidModel.BABY_TRANSFORMER` - the way `@ref=` names an enum constant. **Two live consumers, both armour-stand-small.** `CowModel` and `PigModel` declare a `BabyModelTransform` constant each and **nothing reads either**: `createRoots` registers `COW_BABY` / `PIG_BABY` against hand-built `BabyCowModel` / `BabyPigModel` meshes. `ElytraModel.BABY_TRANSFORMER` and `HappyGhastModel.BABY_TRANSFORMER` share the name but are `scaling(0.5f)` / `scaling(0.2375f)`.

### Session-refresh checklist
1. `git log --oneline master..HEAD | head -30` for branch state.
2. Look at a single entity's failure: `cache/visual/entity-parity-vanilla/<entity_id>/diff_panel.png`.

Walk back from the WRITE log to the texel + shade + blend that produced the mismatch.
